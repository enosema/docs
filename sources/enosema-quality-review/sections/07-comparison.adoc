[[comparison]]
== Comparison of the two corpora

=== The systems compared

The two corpora embody two answers to the same question — how an
international vocabulary should be built and kept — and their
comparison is the most consequential part of this review:

[cols="2,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Dimension
|ISO OBP corpus
|IEV corpus

|production model
|document production: committees write terms and definitions clauses
|vocabulary maintenance: one committee maintains one vocabulary

|authoritative languages
|English (per document)
|English and French, as parallel authorities

|localization model
|definition-bearing per document; translation at adoption
|definition-bearing (eng, fra) plus term-only (17 languages)

|unit of authority
|the document
|the concept record

|uniformity mechanisms
|the drafting rules (ISO/IEC Directives Part 2)
|the maintenance process of TC 1
|===

=== Where the systems agree

Four agreements stand out, and each strengthens the calibration of
the ES requirements:

* *the definition length norm.* ISO median 16 words; IEV English
median 18, French 19. Two independent systems converge on the same
working length. The length guidance of EFS 2522 — genus first, one
to two delimiting characteristics, no padding — describes a norm
that the international system already lives.

* *definitions describe.* Requirements language inside definitions
is 0,35 % (ISO) and effectively zero (IEV). Both systems keep the
boundary between prescribing and defining. The rule that a
definition states what the concept is, not what shall be done with
it, is followed almost perfectly at scale.

* *term type is uniform.* Both corpora record term type at 100 %
with `expression` as the working default. Type annotation is a
solved problem where the record format carries it.

* *presence of the defining element is achievable.* The IEV holds
100 % definition presence in its authoritative languages across all
eras; the ISO modern era approaches it at 99,8 %. Universal
definition presence is not an aspiration beyond the practice of
international bodies; it is their demonstrated modern state.

=== Where the systems differ

* *examples.* ISO 2,9 %; IEV 0,1 %. Neither system produces
examples, and the vocabulary-maintenance system produces fewer than
the document-production system. The example deficit is systemic —
it is the single largest gap between current international practice
and the ES entry-quality requirements, which treat examples as a
scored element.

* *notes.* ISO 34,2 % of entries; IEV 5,4 %. The document culture
annotates; the vocabulary culture concentrates annotation in the
authoritative languages. Notes travel poorly across a term-only
localization layer, because a note written against a definition has
no place in a designation record — the structural reason for the
gap.

* *status richness.* ISO records admitted and deprecated statuses in
the source documents; the IEV extraction carries a single uniform
`preferred`. The lifecycle information that lets a consumer know
which designation to avoid is present in one system and absent in
the other.

* *structural discipline.* Circularity is measurable only in the
ISO corpus (3,5 %, falling by era); the IEV authoritative
definitions were not separately scored for circularity in this
review cycle, and the review notes the gap in its own coverage
(clause <<reproduction>>).

=== The two localization models

The IEV corpus is the empirical demonstration of the two
localization models that the ES series names:

* *the definition-bearing model*: each localization carries its own
definition; equivalence is recorded; review covers terminology
method as well as language. The IEV's English–French pair is a
functioning instance at scale — two parallel authorities, each
complete.

* *the term-only model*: the authoritative definitions serve all
localizations; each localization carries designations, status and
conversion data. The IEV's seventeen other languages are a
functioning instance at scale — median 12 languages per concept
without a single localized definition.

The comparison yields the practical finding that EFG 2519 builds on:
*both models work at international scale*, and the choice between
them is a governance choice — how much definition authoring a
language community can sustain — not a correctness choice. The
failure modes differ: the definition-bearing model fails through
drift between localized definitions and their sources (the
equivalence problem), and the term-only model fails through
designation fit (the position problem). The register declares its
model and reviews for its model's failure mode.

=== A decision table for registers

The comparison converts into the choice that a register operator
faces, with the corpus evidence for each branch:

[cols="2,3,4",options="header",width="100%"]
. Localization model selection by register situation
|===
|Register situation
|Model indicated
|Corpus evidence

|One maintenance committee, definitions authoritative in one or two
languages, translation capacity limited
|term-only
|IEV non-authoritative languages: 17 languages sustained at median
12 per concept without any localized definitions

|Two or more language communities able to author definitions
independently
|definition-bearing, per language
|IEV eng–fra pair: 100 % presence in both, parallel length norms,
no drift signal in the sample

|Localization community with terminology competence and a reviewed
equivalence process
|definition-bearing, with recorded equivalence
|the EFS 2504 apparatus; no corpus instance at scale beyond two
languages — the standing gap of this clause

|Mixed capacity across languages
|declared model per register, never mixed silently
|the Russian residue: partial definition authoring under a
term-only declaration is detectable but ungoverned
|===

The table's last row is the finding that generalizes: partial
adoption of the other model happens whether or not it is declared —
the corpus carries 21 live instances — and the governance question
is whether the register knows. The ES requirement that a register
declare its localization model exists because the alternative is
not "no model" but "an undeclared one".

=== What a third system would add

The comparison is complete for two systems, and the review states
what it cannot show: a corpus that operates the definition-bearing
model across more than two languages — the practice that EFS 2504
requires equivalence recording for — was not available to this
review cycle. The IEV demonstrates the model at two languages; the
ES series requires the equivalence apparatus precisely where the
model scales past the languages that a maintenance committee can
double-author. The gap is a standing item for the next review.
