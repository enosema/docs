The IEV is a definition-bearing system in exactly two languages and
a term-only system in every other language. That is not a defect;

uniformity is the finding: 2 000 of 2 000 sampled concepts carry
English and French entries, and the entries that lack definitions
lack them by design.

Three findings emerge from the coverage table:

* *a two-tier structure.* Eleven languages cover at least 85 % of
concepts; the remainder trail. The vocabulary grew by tiers, and
the tiers are visible in the coverage.

* *near-universal tiers are real.* Polish, Portuguese, Japanese,
German and Korean each cover over 95 % of concepts —
designation coverage, under the term-only model, across a
quarter-million-entry surface when projected over the full corpus.

* *the thin tail is a register of recency and need.* Czech at 25 and
Dutch at 20 entries are partial adoptions, not declining ones;
their presence at all marks languages being added.

=== The two localization models in one corpus

The IEV operates both localization models that EFS 2522 and EFG 2519
name, inside one vocabulary:

* *the definition-bearing model* for English and French: every entry
carries a definition, and the two sets of definitions are
independently authored — French is not a translation layer but a
parallel authority;

* *the term-only model* for all other languages: each language
carries its designations against the authoritative definitions,
with review concentrated on whether the designation can carry the
intension in that language.

The exception proves the model: Russian carries 21 definitions
among its 190 entries (11,1 %, median 15 words) — a language that
at some point received partial definition authoring and stopped.
The residue is detectable precisely because the declared model is
term-only for Russian: any definition present in a term-only
language is an anomaly, and the corpus carries 21 of them.

distinguishes a living vocabulary from a frozen export. The
coverage gaps within the broad tier are uneven across subject
parts — the electrotechnical core parts are covered more deeply
than the peripheral ones — which is the pattern a
need-driven translation programme leaves, and the pattern that
EFG 2519's placement-need records make explicit when a register
operates deliberately.

=== The authoritative pair in detail

The English–French pair is the corpus's definition-bearing heart,
and its statistics describe the model at its best:

* *presence*: 2 000 of 2 000 concepts carry each — zero gaps in the
sample, at every era, in every subject part;

* *length*: English median 18 words, French median 19 — one word
apart, and both one to three words above the ISO corpus median,
which is the convergence finding of clause <<comparison>>;

* *notes*: the overwhelming majority of the corpus's 1 285 notes
attach to the authoritative entries; the note layer serves the
definitions, and the definitions live in two languages only; and

* *independence*: the French definitions are authored against the
French usage of electrotechnical vocabulary, not derived by
translation default — the corpus gives no mechanical translation
signal, and the near-equal length distributions are consistent
with parallel authoring under a common norm.

For a register choosing the definition-bearing model, the pair is
the evidence that the model scales to two languages without
drift — provided the parallel authority is genuinely parallel. The
model's cost is visible in the pair's exclusivity: no third
language sustains definition authoring in the record set, which is
the boundary that EFG 2519's model-declaration doctrine expects a
register to know about itself.

=== A term-only entry set, anatomized

One concept record shows the term-only layer in its ordinary form:

----
# concept 102-01-01, language entries beyond the authoritative pair
pol:
  terms:
    - type: expression
      normative_status: preferred
      designation: równość
  language_code: pol
  entry_status: Standard
  date_accepted: '2009-11-01'
jpn:
  terms:
    - type: expression
      normative_status: preferred
      designation: 等しいこと, 等式
  language_code: jpn
  date_accepted: '2011-05-01'
----

Each entry carries exactly what the term-only model requires: the
designation set with type and status, the language code, the
acceptance date. What it does not carry — a definition, notes, an
equivalence note — is what the model assigns to the authoritative
layer. The review's measures respect the division: presence rates
for the entry elements are computed with the model in view, and the
absence of a definition in `pol` is not scored as an empty
definition but read as the declared form.

The anatomy also shows the model's audit surface: each term-only
entry names its reviewer's question — *can this designation carry
the intension of the authoritative definition in this language?* —
and carries no field for the answer beyond the designation itself.
The equivalence worksheet of EFG 2519 is the instrument that makes
the answer explicit when the question is raised, and the corpus
shows the question raised rarely: the designations are old,
accepted, and stable.

Unlike the ISO corpus, the IEV shows no era gradient in definition
presence — English and French definitions are universal at every
era. The maintenance model, in which one committee owns the
authoritative entries continuously, produces what the ISO
document-production model can only approach: uniform presence of
the defining elements. The era gradient that the ISO corpus shows
for presence is a gradient of the production system, not of
multilingual maintenance.

=== What the IEV corpus lacks

The uniform strengths have a uniform cost, visible in what the
entries lack:

* *examples are absent* (0,1 %) — the term-only model concentrates
effort on designations, and no layer of the vocabulary produces
examples;

* *notes are thin* (5,4 % of entries) — concentrated in the
authoritative languages, where they serve the definitions; the
designations of the term-only languages carry almost no notes;

* *grammar information is absent* — the current record format does
not carry it; and

* *normative status is uniformly `preferred`* — one value across
23 936 entries, which is uniformity of a form that carries no
information: the admission, deprecation and replacement lifecycle
that ISO practice records is not exercised in the corpus as
extracted.

These lacks are the mirror of the ISO findings: where the
production system under-produces (examples), the maintenance system
under-produces more; where the production system is uniform
(sources, status), the maintenance system matches or exceeds it.
