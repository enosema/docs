The system that the OBP corpus represents is strong where the
drafting rules have teeth: definitions are present, sources are
recorded, term types are uniform. It is weak where the rules leave
discretion: examples are rare, and the structural discipline
measures below show the residue of discretionary practice.

The full histogram appears in Annex A. The shape of the distribution
carries three findings:

* *the working norm is tight.* Half of the definitions fall between
roughly 11 and 22 words; the modal bucket is 11–16 words. The
system has an implicit length norm despite no rule fixing a length,
and the norm sits close to the 15-to-20-word guidance that
terminology practice recommends.

* *very short definitions persist.* 214 definitions (4,6 %) are five
words or fewer, and 803 (17,4 %) are ten words or fewer. Short
definitions are legitimate where the genus plus one delimiter
suffices; the review's circularity analysis (below) shows that the
shortest bucket carries a disproportionate share of the circular
definitions.

* *a long tail exists.* 385 definitions (8,4 %) exceed 32 words, and
73 exceed 50. The long definitions are concentrated in documents
that define complex systems; they are not defective by length
alone, and the review treats length as context rather than as a
defect.

=== Structural discipline

The structural measures test whether definitions follow the
genus-differentia discipline:

[cols="3,2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Measure
|Rate
|Reading

|definitional circularity
|3,5 %
|the designation or its head appears in the definition

|requirements language in definitions
|0,35 %
|definitions almost never prescribe

|article-headed definitions
|4,3 %
|a small residue of prose-style openings
|===

The circularity rate is the headline structural finding. At 3,5 %
of definition-bearing entries, roughly one definition in twenty-nine
restates its own designation instead of replacing it — the defect
that the substitution test of EFS 2522 exists to catch. Clause
<<iso-era>> shows the rate falling by era: the discipline is being
learned.

The requirements-language rate is the system's quiet success:
definitions describe, and the near-zero rate of shall, should and
must inside definitions shows that the boundary between normative
clauses and terminology clauses holds across the corpus.

The article-head rate marks the residue of prose-style definitions —
"`A device that...`" openings that bury the genus under the article.
At 4,3 % the residue is small but persistent, and it does not fall
by era (clause <<iso-era>>), which marks it as a drafting habit
rather than a regime effect.

The contrast between notes at a third of entries and examples at

produces explanatory notes far more readily than illustrative
examples. Examples require the author to construct an instance,
which costs more than annotating an existing definition — and the
rate shows the cost is usually not paid.

The era profile is the clearest evidence in the review that the
drafting-rule regimes work:

* *empty definitions collapse.* From 7,7 % in the legacy era to
0,2 % in the modern era — a 38-fold reduction. The legacy residue is
the population of terms published before definitions were enforced;
the modern regime effectively eliminates the defect.

* *circularity halves.* From 4,5–4,8 % in the legacy and
transitional eras to 2,6 % in the modern era. The improvement is
real but incomplete: even under the current regime, one definition
in thirty-eight is circular.

* *the article head persists.* The rate holds roughly steady across
eras, confirming it as a habit rather than a regime effect.

The modern-era residual defects — 0,2 % empty, 2,6 % circular — are
the floor of the current system. They are the numbers that EFS 2522
is calibrated to beat: a conforming termbase should hold empty
definitions near zero and circularity below the modern-era rate.

=== Notes and examples in detail

The notes rate (34,2 %) rewards a closer look, because notes are
the element the drafting culture produces most readily after the
definition itself:

* *notes cluster in the vocabulary standards.* The concentration
table of Annex A shows vocabulary-bearing documents — the
ISO/IEC 2382 series, the 24765 software vocabulary editions, the
5598 and 5593 standards — carrying notes on most entries
(68 of 68, 43 of 61, 24 of 24). These documents maintain notes as
part of their editorial method.

* *notes are rare in domain standards.* The long tail of documents
carrying fewer than twenty entries shows note rates near zero: a
document with twelve definitions typically annotates none of them.
Notes are a practice of vocabulary-maintaining committees, not of
general drafting.

* *the corpus-wide rate is the mixture.* 34,2 % is what the mixture
of a few heavy annotators and many non-annotators yields. The
figure should be read as "the system carries a notes culture in
part of its population", not as "a third of all committees
annotate".

The examples rate (2,9 %) decomposes the same way and worse: the
135 sampled entries with examples concentrate almost entirely in
the vocabulary standards. Outside those, the example is
essentially extinct: the drafting rules carry examples as an
optional element, and optional elements without gates go unused.

=== Circularity in detail

The 161 circular entries (3,5 %) distribute across forms, and the
forms carry different lessons:

* *whole-designation restatement* — the definition begins with or
contains the full designation, giving the substitution test
nothing: the smallest class, and the classic defect;

* *head-noun echo* — the definition contains the designation's head
noun in its defining genus phrase, where the genus is stated and
the differentia repeats the term: the largest class, and the one
the broad rule R3 over-counts most, since legitimate genus
mentions collide with the designation's head; and

* *stem echo* — a derivational stem of the designation recurs
where the concept genuinely differs: the boundary between
unavoidable vocabulary overlap and true circularity, and the
reason the review states the rule as an upper bound.

The era fall (4,5–4,8 % to 2,6 %) is consistent across the forms;
the discipline improved overall rather than one form disappearing.
The modern-era residue concentrates in the head-noun echo class,
which is the form that review — rather than authoring skill —
catches: an author who states the genus honestly collides with the
head noun by accident, and the substitution test at review is the
net that catches the accident. The placement of the substitution
test at review in EFS 2522 is calibrated to exactly this residue.

=== Document concentration

Vocabulary production is concentrated. The twenty largest source
documents in the sample carry 489 entries — 10,5 % of the sample —
and the concentration carries the defects unevenly: five of the
twenty documents carry circular definitions, and the largest single
carrier of circularity in the sample is a single modern-era
systems-integration document. The full table appears in Annex A;
the finding is structural: *the corpus is a small number of large
vocabularies plus a long tail of small ones*, and quality practice
in a handful of committees moves the corpus-wide rate.

=== Grammar information

The extraction carries no grammar information: part of speech,
gender and inflection are absent from the records as extracted. The
finding is bounded: the published platform renders grammar data for
entries that carry it in the source documents, and the absence here
measures the extraction's field coverage rather than the system's
practice. EFS 2522 requires grammar information in conforming
termbases; this corpus cannot benchmark it, and the IEV corpus
(clause <<iev-findings>>) cannot either, for the same reason in its
current record format.

=== What the ISO corpus does not carry

Three absences bound what the corpus can benchmark:

* *no relation structure.* The extraction carries concept records
without the cross-entry relations that the source documents express
implicitly by entry order. Arrangement quality is not measurable in
this corpus; the evidence for arrangement findings comes from the
IEV structure and from the corpus's rake behaviour as published.

* *no grammar fields.* Stated once in clause <<iso-findings>> and
repeated here as a coverage bound: rule R8 measures absence, and
the absence is the extraction's, not the practice's.

* *no lifecycle history.* The records carry acceptance dates, not
revision histories. The era profile is the review's only temporal
instrument, and it measures cohorts, not trajectories; a future
extraction that carries revision chains would let the review
measure movement rather than cohorts.
