[[corpora]]
== The corpora

=== The ISO Online Browsing Platform corpus

The ISO corpus is the terminology content of the ISO Online Browsing
Platform (OBP), extracted into Glossarist-form concept records. The
extraction comprises 406 898 machine-extracted records covering the
published terminology of hundreds of ISO documents — international
standards, technical specifications and technical reports that carry
terms and definitions clauses — from 1983 to the date of extraction.

The corpus as extracted has known characteristics that the review
treats as part of its object of study rather than as noise:

* the records are machine-derived from published documents, and the
extraction fidelity varies with the age and format of the source;

* the records carry the entry structure of the source documents:
definitions, notes, examples, sources and term designations where the
published document carries them; and

* the records carry term-level metadata — term type and normative
status — at the uniformity the platform maintains.

The analysed sample is 4 662 localized concept entries drawn by the
sampling design of clause <<methodology>>.

=== The IEC International Electrotechnical Vocabulary corpus

The IEV corpus is the International Electrotechnical Vocabulary as
maintained by IEC Technical Committee 1 in Glossarist form: one
record per concept, each carrying the concept's language entries. At
extraction the corpus comprises 22 229 concept records. The analysed
sample is 2 000 concept records drawn by the sampling design of
clause <<methodology>>, carrying 23 936 localized concept entries.

The corpus has the structure of a maintained multilingual vocabulary:

* every concept carries an English entry and, except for a residual
set, a French entry, each with a definition — the authoritative
languages of the IEV;

* the remaining languages carry designations without definitions: the
term-only model of localization (clause <<comparison>>);

* each concept carries an identification number of the form
`<part>-<section>-<item>` that places it in a subject part of the
vocabulary; and

* each language entry carries acceptance and review dates that place
the concept in time.

=== Record anatomy

Both corpora arrive in Glossarist form, and the review's measures
run directly over the record fields. The anatomies below show the
shapes; the field names are the ones the rules of Annex C read.

*An ISO record (abridged):*

----
termid: iso-2382-01
localized_concepts:
  eng:
    terms:
      - type: expression
        normative_status: preferred
        designation: central processing unit
    definition:
      - content: "part of a computer that carries out arithmetic
          and logical operations and controls the execution of
          instructions"
    notes:
      - "In some contexts the term is taken to include the primary
          storage."
    examples: []
    source: ISO/IEC 2382:2015, 2126478
    language_code: eng
    entry_status: Standard
    date_accepted: '2018-05-01'
----

The fields the analysis reads: `terms` (type, normative_status,
designation — rules R8, R9), `definition` (rules R1 to R5),
`notes` and `examples` (rule R6), `source` (rule R7), and the
document identity that carries the era assignment (rule R10).

*An IEV record (abridged):*

----
termid: 102-01-01
term: equality
eng:
  id: 102-01-01
  terms:
    - type: expression
      normative_status: preferred
      designation: equality
  definition: "relation between two entities a and b having the
    properties of reflexivity, symmetry and transitivity"
  notes:
    - "The equality of two entities a and b is denoted by a = b."
  language_code: eng
  entry_status: Standard
  date_accepted: '2008-08-01'
fra:
  id: 102-01-01
  terms:
    - type: expression
      normative_status: preferred
      designation: égalité
  definition: "relation entre deux entités a et b..."
  language_code: fra
  date_accepted: '2007-08-01'
pol:
  id: 102-01-01
  terms:
    - type: expression
      normative_status: preferred
      designation: równość
  language_code: pol
  date_accepted: '2009-11-01'
----

The IEV shape differs structurally from the ISO shape: language
keys are top-level, the authoritative pair carries the definitions,
and the remaining languages carry only the designation block. Rule
R12 counts the language keys with designations; the entry-level
measures run per language key.

The scale ratio shapes the sampling design: the ISO corpus is
sampled by entry across a document-dominated population; the IEV
corpus is sampled by concept across a language-dominated one. The
two designs measure the two different things the clause above
names, and the designs are part of the findings' conditions.

=== Third-party content statement

The corpora are the published vocabulary of ISO and of IEC TC 1
respectively. The ES research environment holds the extractions for
analysis; this report publishes statistics, findings and rule
definitions — not corpus content beyond the minimal abridged
anatomy records above, which serve to document the format. Source
documents and full records are not reproduced here, and the
reproduction protocol of Annex E presumes access to the corpora
under their respective terms.

=== What each corpus can and cannot show

The two corpora measure different things well, and the review uses
each for what it measures:

* The ISO corpus measures *the production system*: what the
document drafting process yields when hundreds of committees write
terminology under a common rulebook. Its findings are findings about
authoring practice at scale.

* The IEV corpus measures *the maintenance system*: what one
continuously maintained vocabulary looks like when one body keeps
definitions authoritative and distributes designations. Its findings
are findings about localization architecture and about the
long-run stability of an entry base.

Neither corpus measures conformance to any ES document; the ES
documents did not exist when the corpora were formed. The review
measures the corpora against the measurement rules of clause
<<detection-rules>> and states the implications for the ES
requirements, which are calibrated against this evidence rather than
derived from it.
