[[anti-patterns]]
== Anti-pattern census

=== The census method

EFS 2522, Annex E, names twelve anti-patterns of concept entry
authoring. The census counts their occurrence in the ISO sample
where the detection rules of clause <<detection-rules>> can measure
them from the records. Three anti-patterns require context that the
records do not carry and are marked not measurable in this cycle;
the census therefore reports nine of the twelve.

The census reports rates in the ISO sample and, where the IEV
record format exposes the pattern, in the IEV entries. A rate of a
pattern in the records as extracted measures the pattern in the
extraction; clause <<methodology>> states the extraction limitation
once and this clause does not repeat it.

=== What the census shows

Three conclusions follow from the census as a whole:

* *The rare patterns are the ruled ones.* The patterns that the
drafting rules enforce hardest — sources, prescribed language in
definitions — are at or near zero. The patterns that depend on
author initiative — examples, notes — are far from their ES
targets. Enforced behaviour holds; discretionary behaviour lapses.

* *The era gradient is the enforcement gradient.* Every pattern
with a strong era signal (AP-01, AP-02) tracks a rule that
tightened; every pattern without one (AP-04) tracks a habit no rule
addresses. The census is evidence for the review's wider
proposition: quality follows what the process gates, not what the
manuals recommend.

* *The unmeasured third is a tooling agenda.* AP-07, AP-09, AP-10
and AP-12 need arrangement-level or document-context tooling that
the current review lacks. They are the worklist for the next
cycle, and their absence from the census is stated so that the
census cannot be read as all-clear.

=== The IEV entries against the census

The IEV record format exposes three census patterns:

* *AP-01 (empty definition)*: by design — the term-only entries are
not defects; the declared model decides (clause <<comparison>>).

* *AP-05 (missing examples)*: 99,9 % — deeper than the ISO corpus,
for the structural reason that the term-only layer carries no
definition for an example to attach to.

* *AP-08 (missing notes)*: 94,6 % — notes concentrate in the
authoritative entries; the term-only layer is nearly bare.

The IEV column of the census therefore reads differently from the
ISO column: the same numbers mean different things under different
declared models, and the census distinguishes them rather than
adding them.

=== Reading the census as a register operator

The census rates are not targets to be met individually; they are
the profile of the system whose gates the ES series redesigns. A
register operator reads the census for three uses:

* *as the baseline*. The rates state what unmanaged practice
produces at scale: one entry in twenty-nine circular, one in
twenty-nine empty (legacy-weighted), examples on one entry in
thirty-five. A register's own rates are meaningful only against
this baseline — a 3 % circularity rate is excellent against the
transitional era and weak against the modern one, and the baseline
choice is a governance statement.

* *as the checklist input*. The nine measured patterns map onto the
review checklist of EFS 2522 Annex A in the same order, so a
reviewer can work the checklist knowing how often the corpus fails
at each line — the patterns the corpus fails often deserve the
reviewer's first attention, and the patterns the corpus never
fails (sources) deserve little.

* *as the scoring context*. The EFS 2522 scoring model weights the
dimensions; the census rates show where the corpus mass sits
relative to those weights. The completeness dimension carries 20 %
of the score while the corpus fails it on 97 % of entries — the
weighting is a statement about where a conforming termbase should
differentiate itself from the corpus, and the census is the
quantified statement of that differentiation.

=== Defect interaction

The measured patterns are not independent, and two interactions
matter for interpretation:

* *short definitions and circularity co-occur.* The 1-to-5-word
bucket holds 4,6 % of the definitions but a disproportionate
share of the circular entries: a definition that short rarely has
room for a full delimiting differentia, and the designation's own
words fill it. The interaction says the substitution test matters
most where definitions are tightest — the opposite of the
intuition that short definitions are safe because simple.

* *notes substitute for examples imperfectly.* Entries with notes
carry explanations; entries with examples carry instances. The
corpus's heavy-annotator documents raise notes without moving
examples, which shows the two elements draw on different authoring
efforts and are not interchangeable. A register cannot close the
example gap by annotating more, and the scoring model treats them
separately for exactly this reason.
