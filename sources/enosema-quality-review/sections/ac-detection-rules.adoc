== Detection rules

This annex states the operational definition of every measure. Each
rule is stated so that an implementer can reproduce the count
exactly. The rules were applied uniformly to every entry of the
samples.

=== R1: definition presence

An entry is definition-bearing when its definition field exists and,
after extraction, carries a non-empty text value. A definition field
that is present but empty, or a list field with no content items,
makes the entry term-only.

=== R2: definition length

The definition text is the concatenation of the content items of the
definition field, in order, joined with single spaces. The length is
the count of whitespace-separated tokens. Markup tokens — stem
expressions in AsciiMath or LaTeX notation, inline formatting —
count as tokens; a symbolic definition therefore overstates its
word length, and the finding of clause <<iso-findings>> treats
length as context, not as a defect measure.

=== R3: circularity

An entry is circular when the lowercase form of the preferred
designation (or, for multi-word designations, any word of the
designation of length four or more) occurs within the lowercase
definition text. The rule is deliberately broad: it counts
"`terrain analysis: analysis of terrain`" and "
"`drain: to drain`" alike. A stricter rule would demand the full
designation; the reported rate is an upper bound on true
circularity and a lower bound on agreement, and the same rule was
applied to every era, which is what the era comparison consumes.

=== R4: requirements language

An entry carries requirements language when its definition text
contains any of shall, should or must as a word, case-insensitive.
The rule does not parse modality; quoted uses count. At the
observed rates the distinction is immaterial to the finding.

=== R5: article head

An entry is article-headed when its definition text, after leading
whitespace, begins with a, an or the followed by a space,
case-insensitive.

=== R6: notes and examples presence

An entry carries notes when its notes field carries one or more
non-empty items; the same rule applies to examples.

=== R7: sources presence

An entry carries sources when its source field carries one or more
items. The rule does not validate the cited document.

=== R8: grammar information

An entry carries grammar information when its designation record
carries a non-empty grammar field (part of speech, or gender or
plurality). The rate measures the record set, not the practice that
produced the source documents.

=== R9: term type and normative status

The term type is read from the designation record's type field; the
normative status from the designation record's status field. The
values are reported as found, without normalization beyond the
extraction's own.

=== R10: era assignment (ISO)

The era of an entry is the era of its source document, assigned by
the latest four-digit year appearing in the source document key.
The assignment is coarse; clause <<methodology>> states the
limitation.

=== R11: era assignment (IEV)

The era of a concept is assigned from the date accepted of its
English entry.

=== R12: languages per concept (IEV)

The count is the number of language keys of the concept record that
carry at least one designation. Empty language keys do not count.

=== Worked examples of the rules

The examples show the rules applied, so that an implementer can
calibrate against the intended behaviour rather than the prose.

*R2 applied:* the definition "`part of a computer that carries out
arithmetic and logical operations and controls the execution of
instructions`" yields 17 tokens. A definition containing a stem
expression yields one token per stem symbol — the measure's known
overstatement, stated in the rule.

*R3 applied:* designation `drain valve`, definition "`valve for
draining fluid from a vessel`". The head noun `valve` (length five)
occurs in the definition; rule R3 marks the entry circular, although
the definition states a proper genus-plus-differentia form. The rule
trades precision for coverage, uniformly; the census and the era
profile consume the uniform overcount.

*R3 not applied:* designation `drain`, definition "`to remove fluid
by drainage`" — the word `drain` does not occur (the definition
carries `drainage`, a different token under exact matching), so the
rule does not mark it; the stem echo class of clause <<iso-findings>>
names this boundary case.

*R5 applied:* definition "`A device that measures voltage`" begins
with `A ` — article-headed. Definition "`Device for measuring
voltage`" does not — the genus stands first, and the entry passes.

*R6 applied:* a notes field of one non-empty item counts; an empty
list does not; a list of one empty string does not.

*R10 applied:* source key `iso.10303-2.2024` carries the latest year
2024; the era is modern. Key `iso.7348.1992` carries 1992; the era
is legacy.

=== Rule change policy

A rule change between editions invalidates the affected trend
comparisons, and the review constrains changes accordingly:

* a rule may be tightened or loosened only in a new edition, with
the change stated in that edition and both rates reported for the
first edition in which both are computable;

* era comparisons within one edition use one rule set, without
exception — the era findings consume uniformity, and a mixed rule
set would manufacture or mask gradients; and

* the rules of this annex are the baseline: any future change is
stated as a diff against them, not as a replacement text.

The policy exists because the review's authority rests on
reproducibility; a rule that changes silently between editions
would make every trend line negotiable.
