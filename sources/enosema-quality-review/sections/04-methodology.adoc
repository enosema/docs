[[methodology]]
== Methodology

=== Sampling design

Both corpora are too large to analyse entry by entry in this review,
and the review draws probability samples with fixed seeds:

* *ISO corpus*: a simple random sample of 4 662 localized concept
entries, drawn from the 406 898 extracted records with seed 2522. The
sample size was set by the analysis capacity of the review cycle and
yields rate estimates with a width of approximately ±1,4 percentage
points at the 95 % level for rates near 50 %, and tighter for the
extreme rates that dominate the findings.

* *IEV corpus*: a simple random sample of 2 000 concept records,
drawn from the 22 229 records with seed 2522, analysed exhaustively
across all language entries of each sampled concept. The concept is
the sampling unit and the language entry the analysis unit, which
preserves the multilingual structure in the sample.

Both seeds are recorded in Annex E. Re-running the analysis with the
same seeds reproduces every figure in this report; re-running with
different seeds shifts rates within the sampling error stated above.

The sample sizes are sized to the decisions that the review supports.
The ES requirements are calibrated to differences between eras,
between corpora and between element rates — differences of five
percentage points and more — and a sample that resolves one-point
differences is more than the calibration needs. The ISO sample of
4 662 entries resolves half-point differences at the headline rates
(<<a-sampling-error>>), which bounds the sampling error well below the
smallest difference any finding turns on; the IEV sample of 2 000
concepts resolves one-point differences at the concept level and
yielded language-level rates with ample depth, since every sampled
concept contributes its full language set. Growing the samples
would sharpen rates that no finding consumes; the sizes in force
match evidence to decision.

=== Measures

The review measures each localized concept entry on the following
variables. Clause <<detection-rules>> states each rule verbatim.

[cols="2,4,2",options="header"]
|===
|Variable
|Meaning
|Applies to

|definition presence
|the entry carries a non-empty definition
|both corpora

|definition length
|token count of the definition text
|definition-bearing entries

|circularity
|the designation (or a distinctive part of it) appears in its own
definition
|definition-bearing entries

|requirements language
|the definition contains shall, should or must
|definition-bearing entries

|article head

|definition-bearing entries

|the entry carries one or more notes
|both corpora

|the designation carries part-of-speech and inflection data

|===

=== Derived profiles

Three derived profiles organize the findings:

* *the length distribution*: definition lengths bucketed at fixed
cut points (1–5, 6–10, 11–16, 17–22, 23–32, 33–50, 51 and more
words), with median, mean and the 10th and 90th percentiles;

* *the era profile*: each variable aggregated by document era as
defined in clause <<terms-report>>, which shows how practice moved
with the drafting-rule regimes; and

* *the concentration profile*: the share of entries and of defects
carried by the largest source documents (ISO) and the share of
entries carried by each language (IEV).

=== Limitations

The review states four limitations that bound its conclusions:

* *extraction fidelity*. The corpora are machine-derived; the
extraction inherits the defects of the source formats. The rates
reported here are rates in the records as extracted, which for the
ISO corpus may differ from the published documents in formatting
detail.

* *rule sensitivity*. Each defect rate is conditional on its
detection rule; a stricter circularity rule lowers the rate, a
looser one raises it. The rules are stated verbatim so that this
sensitivity is visible rather than hidden.

* *sample size*. The rates carry sampling error; the review quotes
the error bounds where a finding rests on a small difference.

* *era attribution*. The ISO era profile assigns each entry the era
of its source document's latest year; a document revised across
regimes is assigned one era. The attribution is coarse and the
era findings are directional, not exact.

None of the limitations weakens the findings that rest on large
differences between corpora or between eras; they bound the
precision, not the direction, of the results.
