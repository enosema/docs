[[binary-relations]]
== Binary relations

=== When to use a binary relation

A binary relation is the correct assertion when the relation holds between
exactly two concepts and neither the joint significance of a group of
relations, nor the completeness of a decomposition, nor per-member
presence or count is being asserted.

Binary relations express:

* hierarchical membership of one concept under another
(<<rel-hierarchical>>);

* pairwise associative links of a stated kind (<<rel-associative>>);

* equivalence and mapping between two concepts (<<rel-equivalence>>); and

* lifecycle and register assertions between two concepts
(<<rel-other>>).

=== Recording direction and inverse

A binary relation assertion shall record:

. the relation type identifier;

. the direction, by the ordered pair (current concept, related concept)
per the semantics of the type; and

. optionally, an explanatory content string clarifying the specific
assertion.

Where a system materializes inverses, the inverse relation type shall be
the one specified in <<rel-vocabulary>>. Systems shall not invent inverse
identifiers.

=== Assertions between designations

Some relation types apply between specific *designations* of concepts
rather than between concepts — for example, that one designation is an
abbreviated form for another. These designation-level relations use the
same vocabulary, restricted to the types whose semantics apply to
designations:

* `abbreviated_form_for`: the current designation is an abbreviated form
of the related designation of the same concept;

* `short_form_for`: the current designation is a short form of the related
designation of the same concept;

* `full_form_for`: the current designation is the full form of the related
abbreviation.

Concept management systems shall record designation-level relations on the
designation record and shall not encode them as concept-level relations.

=== Informative versus normative relations

Relations in a concept system differ in normative force:

* structural relations (hierarchical, partitive, instantial) are
*normative*: they define the intension and extension of concepts and shall
be validated for coherence (<<quality>>);

* associative relations are *informative* by default: they explain and
connect, but do not delimit. A domain can declare an associative kind
normative for its purposes (for example, `causes` in a safety domain);
such declarations belong to the domain profile, not to this document.
