[[concept-systems]]
== Concept systems

=== General

Concepts are managed as systems, not in isolation. A concept system arranges
concepts according to their relations so that each concept is positioned by
its intension (its characteristics) and its extension (its instances), and
each term assigned to a concept is motivated by that position.

Concept systems serve three purposes:

* they expose gaps: a missing subordinate concept within a subdivision
signals incomplete terminology;

* they expose overlaps: two concepts sharing the same position in a
subdivision signal redundancy;

* they support derivation: definitions, term formation, and entries in
terminological collections are drafted from the position and relations of
each concept.

=== Concepts and characteristics

A concept is constituted by a unique combination of characteristics.

*Characteristics* are of two kinds:

* *inherent characteristics*, which apply to every instance of the concept
and make the concept what it is; and

* *delimiting characteristics* (<<term-delimiting-characteristic>>), which
distinguish the concept from its coordinate concepts within one subdivision.

In a subdivision, each coordinate concept shall be delimited from the
others by at least one delimiting characteristic. Where a coordinate
concept has no delimiting characteristic, the subdivision is not MECE
(mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive) and shall be reworked
either by refining the criterion of subdivision or by merging the
coordinate concepts.

=== Concept coordination

Concepts are coordinated — brought into a system — by three canonical
coordination methods. Every relation in a concept system is grounded in one
of these methods or is associative (<<rel-associative>>).

[[coord-generic]]
==== Generic coordination

Generic coordination subdivides a concept into subordinate concepts that are
*kinds* of it: each subordinate concept inherits the intension of the
superordinate concept and adds at least one delimiting characteristic.

[example]
In generic coordination, the concept *signal* (information transfer) is
subdivided into *analog signal* and *digital signal*, by the delimiting
characteristic of value continuity.

Generic coordination forms the *genus-differentia* structure of definitions:
the superordinate concept provides the genus and the delimiting
characteristics provide the differentiae.

[[coord-partitive]]
==== Partitive coordination

Partitive coordination subdivides a concept into subordinate concepts that
are *parts* of it, where the parts fitted together constitute the whole.

Partitive coordination is legitimate only when both conditions hold:

. the parts, fitted together, constitute the whole; and

. the parts are coordinate: they share the comprehensive concept
(<<term-comprehensive-concept>>) and share a criterion of subdivision.

A partitive subdivision with only a single part is not a partitive
coordination; a single part-of assertion is expressed as a binary
partitive relation (<<rel-partitive-binary>>).

[[coord-instantial]]
==== Instantial coordination

Instantial coordination relates individual concepts to the general concept
they realize. An *individual concept* is a concept that corresponds to a
unique instance and is not further subdivided by the criteria of
terminology work.

[example]
The concept * metre* (the SI unit) is an instance of the general concept
*unit of length*; the concept * Protocol for web resource identification*
is general, while the concept *the URN namespace `isbn`* is individual.

[[criteria-multidim-systems]]
=== Criteria of subdivision and multidimensionality

Every subdivision shall be performed with an explicit criterion of
subdivision (<<term-criterion-of-subdivision>>). The criterion shall be
stated in the concept system record and shall remain associated with the
subdivision it generated.

Where more than one criterion is applied to the same set of concepts, the
concept system is *multidimensional* (<<term-multidimensionality>>).
Multidimensionality shall be represented as parallel subdivisions, one per
criterion, and shall not be represented as a single subdivision whose
coordinate concepts are delimited by characteristics drawn from different
criteria.

[example]
The concept *road vehicle* may be subdivided by motive (motorized, human-powered)
and by environment (on-road, off-road). These are two parallel subdivisions.
A single subdivision containing *motorized on-road*, *motorized off-road*,
*human-powered on-road*, and *human-powered off-road* collapses two criteria
into one level and is not a conforming representation.

[NOTE]
====
Multidimensionality also applies to n-ary relations: the same comprehensive
concept may have several rakes of the same kind, distinguished by their
criterion (<<nary-criterion>>). Concept management systems shall preserve
the distinction between such rakes.
====

=== Concept systems as graphs

A concept system is a directed labelled graph: concepts are nodes, concept
relations are labelled edges (binary relations) or labelled hyperedges
(n-ary relations). The graph representation makes the following operations
well-defined and machine-checkable:

* traversal from any concept to its superordinate, subordinate, and
associated concepts;

* validation of the constraints specified in <<quality>>;

* projection into vocabularies such as SKOS, where generic coordination
projects into broader/narrower hierarchies and associative relations
project into skos:related.

Concept systems shall be maintained as directed labelled graphs with
explicit labels; concept management systems shall not infer relation types
from layout, position, or diagram style alone.
