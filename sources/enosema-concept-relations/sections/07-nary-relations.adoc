[[nary-relations]]
== N-ary relations

=== General

An n-ary relation asserts that a group of concepts is jointly significant
with respect to a comprehensive concept. ISO 704 expresses such groups as
*rakes* or *brackets* in concept diagrams: a single line drawn from the
comprehensive concept to two or more coordinate concepts means that the
coordination is joint, not an aggregation of independent pairwise links.

An n-ary relation shall record:

. the *kind* of the relation: partitive, generic, or sequential;

. the *comprehensive concept* (<<term-comprehensive-concept>>);

. the *members*: two or more coordinate concepts, each referencing the
comprehensive concept, in order where order is significant;

. the *completeness* (<<term-completeness>>): `complete` or `partial`;

. the *criterion of subdivision* (<<nary-criterion>>) or *criterion of
sequencing* (<<rel-nary-sequential>>); and

. optional per-member metadata: presence, count, and delimiting status
(<<nary-partitive>>).

=== The binary-versus-n-ary decision rule

A decomposition shall be recorded as an n-ary relation, not as a set of
binary relations, when at least one of the following holds:

. the membership of the group is *jointly significant*: the subordinate
concepts fitted together constitute the comprehensive concept;

. the *completeness* of the group is asserted (the members exhaust the
intension of the comprehensive concept, or explicitly do not);

. *per-member presence or count* is asserted (some members exist in every
instance; some in only some instances; some singly; some multiply); or

. the *delimiting* part is identified among the members.

Otherwise — where the assertion is merely that one concept relates to
another — a binary relation shall be used.

[example]
A *road vehicle* having an *engine* and a *chassis* is a jointly significant
partitive decomposition: a n-ary partitive relation with comprehensive
concept `road vehicle` and members `engine` (required, exactly_one) and
`chassis` (required, exactly_one) is recorded.

[example]
A *publication* having an *author* is a loose partitive association without
joint significance: a binary `has_part`-style relation is recorded, since
publications can have any number of authors and the author does not
delimit the publication.

[[nary-partitive]]
=== Partitive n-ary relations

A partitive n-ary relation (a *partitive rake*) records the decomposition
of a comprehensive concept into the parts that fitted together constitute
it.

Per-member metadata follows the ISO 704 diagram conventions:

* *presence* (<<term-presence>>): `required` — the part exists in every
instance of the comprehensive concept (solid line); `optional` — the part
exists in some instances only (dashed line);

* *count*: `exactly_one` — one instance (single line); `at_least_one` —
one or more instances; `multiple` — two or more instances (double line);

* *delimiting status*: where one member carries the delimiting
characteristic that distinguishes the comprehensive concept within its own
subdivision, it is marked *delimiting* (bold line of three-fold width in
ISO 704 diagrams).

[example]
A *railway switch* consists of a *switch blade* (required, exactly_one,
delimiting), a *switch motor* (optional, exactly_one) and a *locking
device* (required, at_least_one).

The combination of presence and count shall be consistent: an `optional`
member shall not be recorded with `exactly_one` count, since presence is
already conditional.

[[nary-generic]]
=== Generic n-ary relations

A generic n-ary relation records the kinds that jointly constitute a
comprehensive concept under a stated criterion of subdivision. It is used
where the set of kinds is itself significant — for example, to assert that
a classification is exhaustive under one criterion:

* the comprehensive concept is the superordinate concept;

* the members are the kinds, which shall be coordinate
(<<term-coordinate-concept>>): they share the comprehensive concept and
the criterion;

* `completeness: complete` asserts that the kinds exhaust the extension
under the criterion; `completeness: partial` asserts that additional
kinds exist or are anticipated.

Generic n-ary relations carry no per-member presence or count metadata;
each kind either exists in the system or it does not.

[[rel-nary-sequential]]
=== Sequential n-ary relations

A sequential n-ary relation records two or more subordinate concepts
*ordered* by a criterion of sequencing, jointly constituting a
comprehensive concept such as a process, a development, or a lifecycle.

The relation shall record:

. the *criterion of sequencing*: `temporal`, `spatial`, `developmental`,
`causal`, `process_step`, or a domain-defined criterion;

. the members in their order; and

. the completeness of the sequence.

[example]
The development of *amphibian* from *larva* to *adult* is a developmental
sequence; the stages of a *release lifecycle* (proposed, drafted, reviewed,
published) are process steps.

The sequential n-ary relation is preferred over chains of binary
`causes`/`develops_into` edges wherever the order and the joint
significance of the stages matter; binary sequential edges remain correct
for isolated pairwise assertions.

[[nary-criterion]]
=== Criterion records

The criterion of subdivision or sequencing shall be recorded with the
n-ary relation, not inferred. A criterion record carries:

. a human-readable statement of the criterion; and

. an identifier where the criterion is itself a managed item (for example,
a registered subdivision criterion in the domain register).

The same comprehensive concept may carry several n-ary relations of the
same kind distinguished only by criterion; systems shall present these as
parallel decompositions (<<criteria-multidim>>) and shall not merge them.

[[criteria-multidim]]
NOTE: Multidimensionality at the level of concept systems is specified in
<<criteria-multidim-systems>>; the n-ary criterion record applies the same
principle to individual decompositions.

[[nary-storage]]
=== Registration of n-ary relations

Within a register, an n-ary relation is an item in its own right,
identified independently of the concepts it connects:

. its identifier is stable across member edits;

. its lifecycle (proposed, accepted, retired) follows the register
procedures of ISO 19135 as adopted by the FERIN framework;

. member edits are recorded as version changes of the relation item, so
that citing systems can reference the decomposition as it was at a given
time.

Concept management systems shall store n-ary relations as first-class
items and shall not reconstruct them by traversing binary edges, since
the joint significance, completeness, and per-member metadata are not
recoverable from pairwise assertions alone.
