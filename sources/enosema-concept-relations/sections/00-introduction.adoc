== Introduction

Concepts do not exist in isolation. A concept is understood through its
relations to other concepts: the broader concept it is a kind of, the parts
that fit together to constitute it, the instances that realize it, and the
multitude of associative links that relate it to its causes, materials,
actors, and products. The systematic arrangement of concepts according to
their relations forms a *concept system*, and the quality of a concept
system largely determines the quality of the terminology that is derived
from it.

International standards establish the foundations of this work. ISO 704
establishes the principles and methods of terminology work, including the
theory of concept systems and the characteristics of concepts. ISO 1087-1
provides the vocabulary of terminology work. ISO 25964, developed for
thesauri and interoperability with other vocabularies, enumerates the
semantic relationships that connect concepts. ISO 12620 provides a data
category registry whose relation categories are widely implemented.
ISO 10241-1 governs the drafting of terminological entries in standards.
ISO 19135 establishes procedures for the registration of items, the
foundation of the FERIN framework for the registration of information.

In practice, however, organizations that manage concepts face recurring
questions that these standards answer only in fragments:

* which relation types should a concept system distinguish, and what is
the semantics of each;
* when is a pairwise (binary) relation the right assertion, and when is a
joint, multi-member assertion (a "`rake`") required;
* how are completeness, per-member multiplicity, and the criterion of
subdivision recorded so that concept diagrams and their meaning survive
into machine-readable form;
* how are relations managed over time as concepts are extended, split,
instantiated, or retired within a register.

This document answers these questions. It specifies the relation
vocabulary of ES concept systems, the rules for choosing between binary
relations and n-ary relations, the metadata that accompanies multi-member
relations, and the quality constraints that keep a concept system
coherent as it evolves. It is written to be directly implementable: each
relation type is defined with its direction, its inverse, and its
semantic conditions, so that terminology management systems can
enumerate, validate, and interchange concept relations without
ambiguity.

This document is part of a suite of ES specifications for terminology
management. EFS 2501 addresses principles and methods of terminology
management, EFS 2504 addresses multilingual terminology management, and
EFS 2507 addresses the abstract model of concept relationships. This
document builds on those foundations and specifies the relation
vocabulary and system-level practices in full.
