[[introduction]]
== Introduction

=== The problem this document solves

A concept system is only as good as its arrangement. Two termbases can
hold the same concepts with the same definitions and still present
fundamentally different knowledge: in one, every concept sits in a
single defensible position, every subdivision separates its
coordinates cleanly, and every relation carries the evidence that
justifies it; in the other, concepts float in several systems without
recorded reasons, subdivisions overlap so that consumers cannot tell
where a boundary runs, and relations assert positions that no reviewer
has qualified. The difference is not the vocabulary of terms. The
difference is arrangement.

The international framework answers parts of the problem. ISO 704
gives the theory: concepts are bounded by intensions, systems are
built by subdividing along a criterion, and the regrouping test checks
whether a subdivision is sound. ISO 1087-1 defines the relation
categories. EFS 2510 supplies the machine-readable vocabulary of
relation types. None of them states, as operational requirements that
a register can check, how membership is decided and recorded, which
metadata a relationship is required to carry, or which tests a
subdivision is required to pass before it ships.

This document does that. It treats arrangement as a governed process
with four operations, each with its own requirement group:

* *membership* (Group A): the decision that a concept belongs to a
concept system, at a stated position, in one system or in several;

* *relationship establishment* (Group E): the selection, direction and
recording of the relations that connect a concept to its
superordinates, subordinates and associates;

* *relationship qualification* (Group Q): the metadata — source,
criterion, delimitation, count constraints, normative force, status —
that turns an asserted relation into a defensible one; and

* *mutual exclusivity and collective exhaustiveness* (Group M): the
discipline that every subdivision of a superordinate concept separates
its coordinates without overlap and accounts for its full extension.

Group V (arrangement validation) binds the four operations to the
checks and reviews that keep them true over time.

=== Why mutual exclusivity is a requirement, not an aspiration

Mutual exclusivity is often described as a property to hope for. This
document treats it as a property to test for. A subdivision that
overlaps — where one concept satisfies the delimiting characteristics
of two coordinates at once — produces entries whose definitions
contradict each other, produces consumers who cannot decide which
designation to use, and produces relations that assert a position for a
concept that a second relation contradicts. Group M states the tests
that detect these failures while the arrangement is being built, and
the remediation paths that apply when a test fails. The tests are
stated so that most of them can run mechanically over a machine-
readable register (EFS 2516, EFS 2513, clause 7), and so that the
remainder are review tasks with written procedures.

=== Concepts in several systems at once

Real terminology does not stay inside one hierarchy. A concept such as
"data quality" belongs in a management concept system, in a statistics
concept system, and in a domain-specific system, and it occupies a
different position in each. Multidimensional subdivision — one system
per criterion of subdivision, combined by composition — is the
correct instrument when the dimensions are known in advance. Cross-
system membership with mapped positions is the correct instrument when
separate communities maintain separate systems. This document states
requirements for both instruments and the rule for choosing between
them, so that a register records one truth per position instead of
pretending that one hierarchy can serve all viewpoints.

=== How to use this document

A register operator who is building or auditing a concept system reads
<<arrangement>> for the arrangement principles, <<membership>> for
membership, <<establishing>> for establishing relations, <<qualifying>>
for qualifying them, and <<mece>> for the exclusivity discipline; the
annexes carry the working
instruments — the placement decision procedure (Annex A), the MECE
test suite (Annex B), the qualification vocabulary (Annex C), the
worked examples (Annex D), and the cross-reference map to the other ES
terminology documents (Annex E). Requirement identifiers (A, E, Q, M,
V) are cited in the checklists of EFG 2519 and in the scoring model of
EFS 2522 where arrangement quality feeds an entry grade.

=== Relationship to the evidence base

The requirements of this document are calibrated against the same
evidence base as the rest of the ES Terminology series. The corpus
study of international standard vocabulary — the ISO Online Browsing
Platform terminology and the IEC International Electrotechnical
Vocabulary, analysed in the ES quality review of international
standard vocabulary — shows what large production systems actually
maintain: hierarchies dominated by single criteria, positions
asserted without recorded criteria, and the MECE discipline upheld
in places and merely intended in others. The requirement groups of
this document name those practices where they succeed and close them
where they fail:

* the single-criterion test (M-3) and the criterion record (Q-2)
respond to the unrecorded and unnamed criteria that the corpora
exhibit wherever subdividers left their decisions implicit;

* the boundary convention rule (M-7) responds to the ranged
subdivisions — sizes, ranges, durations — whose corpora-wide
boundaries are conventionally applied in practice but stated almost
nowhere;

* the residual category rule (M-6) responds to the "`other`" and
"`miscellaneous`" nodes that the corpora carry at every depth, and
states the discipline that keeps them from accreting; and

* the cross-system requirements (A-9 to A-13) respond to the reality
that international vocabularies overlap across communities, and that
correspondence between positions is a fact to record, not a
coincidence to ignore.

The quality review report of the ES Terminology series carries the
full statistics and the traceability from each finding to the
requirements it informs. The registers that conform to EFS 2513 and
arrange under this document are, among other things, the next
corpus: the validation records of Group V are the evidence by which
arrangement quality becomes observable and improvable over time.

=== How the requirement groups hold together

The five groups divide the work of arrangement along its real seams,
and the seams matter to conformity. A register can hold strong
records (Group E, Group Q) and still ship an overlapping rake
(Group M failure); another can test exclusivity diligently and still
lose the property because membership moves without review (Group A
and Group V failure). Conformance to this document is conformance to
all five groups, and the groups cite each other where the
dependency runs:

* Group A produces the assertions that Group E forms and Group Q
qualifies; a placement decided outside Group A has no Group Q
metadata, because nothing determined its criterion or its source;

* Group M tests what Group A and Group E build, and its remediation
paths route back through Group A procedures and Group Q records; and

* Group V observes the whole: its mechanical checks run the
structural rules of Groups A, E and Q, its reviews run the judgement
tests of Group M, and its records are the evidence that the first
four groups hold.

The identifiers are stable across editions, and the dependencies are
part of the contract: a checklist or a tool that cites M-3 is citing
a test whose inputs are produced by Q-2 records, and a conforming
register supplies both.
