[[membership]]
== System membership (Group A)

=== General

A concept position exists only as a recorded assertion. The position
states that a concept is subordinate to a stated superordinate concept
under a stated criterion of subdivision; the assertion lives in the
register as a relation record (EFS 2516, EFS 2510). Group A governs
the making of that assertion: who decides it, on what evidence, in
which system, and in how many systems.

=== Membership decision

A-1:: System membership shall be asserted only by a hierarchical
relation of the relation vocabulary (EFS 2510): a generic relation or
a partitive relation. An associative relation, a mapping relation, or
a lifecycle relation shall not carry system membership.

A-2:: A membership assertion shall state the concept, the
superordinate concept, the relation type, and the criterion of
subdivision under which the concept is coordinate with the
superordinate's other subordinate concepts.

A-3:: A membership assertion shall be decided against the intensions
of the concepts: the subordinate concept shall presuppose the
intension of the superordinate concept, and shall add at least one
delimiting characteristic that distinguishes it from each of its
coordinate concepts (ISO 704).

A-4:: The membership decision shall be reviewed and accepted by the
control body or its delegate under the change management requirements
of EFS 2513, clause 7, before the position becomes public.

A-5:: Where a membership decision is contested — two proposed
positions both plausibly fit — the register shall record the
contested positions and the decision, with reasons, in the proposal
record (EFG 2519, Annex B); the position that ships is the accepted
one, and the alternative remains discoverable in the record.

=== Membership in one system

A-6:: Within one concept system, a concept shall occupy exactly one
position: exactly one hierarchical relation shall place the concept
under a superordinate concept of that system, except for the root
concepts of the system, which carry none.

A-7:: Where two positions in one system appear equally correct, the
system shall not carry both; the operator applies the placement
decision procedure (Annex A) to select one, and records the rejected
candidate position in the membership record.

A-8:: A concept with no adequate superordinate concept in a system
shall not be forced into an inadequate one; the register holds the
concept as unplaced in that system, with a recorded placement need
under the requirements management of EFS 2513, clause 6, until either
a superordinate concept is added or a subdivision is restructured.

=== Membership in several systems

A-9:: A concept may hold membership in several concept systems. Each
membership is a separate assertion, complete in itself: it states its
own superordinate concept, its own relation type, and its own
criterion of subdivision in the system that receives it.

A-10:: Where the several systems are facets of one arrangement — the
dimensions are known and maintained together — the register shall
represent the arrangement as a multidimensional concept system: each
dimension subdivides by its own criterion, and the positions of the
system are the compositions of the dimensional coordinates.

A-11:: Where the several systems are maintained independently —
different scopes, different control bodies, different vocabularies —
the register shall represent the arrangement as cross-system
membership, and shall connect the positions with mapping relations
per EFS 2510 if a stated correspondence between the positions exists.

A-12:: A register shall not represent one arrangement as both a
multidimensional composition and a set of independent systems: where
the dimensions are co-maintained, clause A-10 applies; where they are
not, clause A-11 applies. The choice is recorded in the system
description (EFS 2513, clause 5).

A-13:: Cross-system membership shall not be used to place one concept
twice within one system. Two positions that differ only in path —
where one is reachable from the other through the system — belong to
one system for the purposes of clause A-6.

=== Membership movement

Concepts move. A definition is tightened, a rake is re-criteried, a
system is restructured — and the position of a concept changes. The
movement is as significant to consumers as the position itself,
because the vacated position may be cited in their systems and the
taken position may contradict their assumptions. Group A governs
movement with three requirements.

*The move is a pair.* A position change is recorded as the vacated
position and the taken position (A-16): the vacated relation is
deprecated with a replacement pointer (Q-10), and the new relation is
asserted through the full placement procedure (Annex A). Consumers
who followed the vacated position can trace where the concept went.

*The reason is recorded.* A move without a recorded reason is a move
that will be reverted by the next well-meaning reviewer. The move
records what changed in the world that made the old position wrong:
the definition change, the re-criterion decision, the scope change.

*The dependents are reviewed.* A move changes the rakes of two
superordinate concepts: the rake that loses a coordinate and the rake
that gains one. Both rakes are re-tested under the MECE suite (T1 to
T6) before publication, because a rake that lost its only
distinguishing coordinate can quietly fail coverage, and a rake that
gained a coordinate can quietly fail exclusivity.

=== Roots, placeholders and orphans

Three boundary cases of membership recur, and the register states
their treatment:

* *root concepts* carry no incoming hierarchical relation (A-6). A
system states its roots in the system description; a system with
several roots states the relation among the roots — usually a
multidimensional composition rather than a hierarchy (A-10).

* *placeholder superordinates* are concepts introduced to give a rake
a home where no natural superordinate concept exists — the "`miscel-
laneous`" node. A placeholder is declared as such in the system
description, carries the residual treatment of M-6, and is a
liability: the register reviews placeholders at every arrangement
review with the question of whether a real criterion now exists to
replace them.

* *orphans* are concepts whose membership relation was deprecated
without a replacement. The register detects orphans mechanically (T1)
and holds them out of the arrangement view until re-placed or retired
through change management. An orphan is never published as a member
of the system, because a member without a position is an arrangement
assertion the register cannot defend.

=== Membership and localization

Membership attaches to concepts, not to language entries: a concept
is placed once, and every localization of the concept inherits the
position (A-9, E-14). The inheritance has two consequences that
registers get wrong:

* *language-specific systems are separate systems.* Where a
language community maintains its own concept system — its own
subdivisions, its own criteria — the system is a distinct concept
system, not a localization of another one, and the correspondence
between the systems is stated with mapping relations (A-11), following
the multilingual requirements of EFS 2504. The register does not
present a language-specific system as the translation of the
authoritative system when the arrangements differ.

* *localization review re-checks position fit.* When a localization
is accepted (EFS 2504; EFG 2519, clause 8), the reviewer confirms
that the localized designation can carry the intension *in its
position* — the concept-system check of the equivalence worksheet. A
designation that fits the intension but not the position signals that
the target-language concept system differs, and the finding is
recorded on the mapping, not on the localization.

=== Membership in multidimensional systems

A multidimensional concept system (A-10) composes the positions of
the system from dimensional coordinates. Membership in such a system
differs from membership in a simple hierarchy in three respects, and
the register records the difference:

* *membership is by composition.* A position in a composed system is
identified by its coordinate values — one per dimension. The concept
holds one membership record per dimension, each carrying the
dimensional rake, and the composed position is derived from the set
of dimensional memberships. A consumer reads the composed position
without the register asserting it separately, which keeps the
composition from being duplicated in records.

* *completeness is per dimension.* The MECE tests run per
dimensional rake (M-11): each dimension partitions on its own
criterion. Coverage of the composed system is the product of the
dimensional coverages where the dimensions are independent; where
two dimensions interact — a combination that the system excludes —
the system states the excluded combinations in the system
description, and the statement is part of the coverage claim (M-5).

* *absence of a coordinate value is a recordable state.* A concept
that is undetermined along one dimension — the currency of a data
set is not yet known — holds no membership record for that dimension,
and the composed position is partial. The register distinguishes a
partial position from a missing record mechanically (T1 flags
concepts that lack a dimensional membership where the dimension
applies to the concept's scope).

The multidimensional choice is structural: converting a flattened
mixed rake into a composed system (the re-dimension path of M-9) is a
change-management event per M-10, because it changes the records that
consumers read.

=== Placement anti-patterns

The placement procedure fails in recognizable ways, and naming the
failures makes them visible in review. Five anti-patterns recur in
registers:

* *the convenient shelf.* A concept is placed where space exists
rather than where the intension leads — the new concept joins the
most recently edited rake because the proposer knew that rake. The
signature is a placement whose tie-breaker is absent: only one
candidate was examined. The remedy is procedural (V-5 re-derives
positions) and cultural: proposals carry the candidate enumeration,
and a single-candidate enumeration is a review finding.

* *the historical sediment.* A concept stays under a superordinate it
outgrew because it has always been there. The signature is a
membership whose source predates every revision of the definition it
places. The remedy is the event trigger of V-4: definition revisions
queue the affected memberships for review, and stale placements
surface on schedule.

* *the mirror placement.* A concept is duplicated into a second
system wholesale — same rake, same criterion, same definition —
because a community wanted "its own copy". The signature is two
systems whose mapping relations are all `equivalence` while their
criteria differ in name only. The remedy is A-11 discipline: one
maintained arrangement, the second system relating by mapping; or a
genuinely distinct system whose differences are stated.

* *the orphaned branch.* A subtree's root is re-placed and its
descendants are not re-tested. The signature is a rake that passes
T1 while failing T6 — structurally present, no longer covered. The
remedy is M-12 discipline: parent remediation triggers child retest,
and the validation record shows the retest.

* *the loaded placeholder.* A placeholder superordinate accumulates
coordinates until it is a real taxonomy nobody declared. The
signature is a placeholder whose rake exceeds the breadth guideline
(T13) or whose residual definition has grown positive content. The
remedy is the placeholder review of clause <<membership>>, and the
honest options are to found a real dimension or to split the
placeholder by re-criterion.

The anti-patterns inform review; they do not replace the tests. A
register can carry every anti-pattern signature and still pass its
MECE suite, and a placement that matches none of the signatures can
still fail T3. The tests decide; the patterns speed the look.

=== Membership record

A-14:: Each membership assertion shall be recorded as a relation
record per EFS 2516 carrying, as a minimum: the relation type, the
subject and object concepts, the criterion of subdivision, the
qualifying metadata of clause <<qualifying>>, and the lifecycle status
of the assertion.

A-15:: The membership record shall be machine-readable in the
published register (EFS 2513, clause 10): a consumer shall be able to
reconstruct the system — positions, rakes and criteria — from the
published relation records without inspecting prose.

A-16:: A change to a membership assertion — a move of position, a
change of criterion, a change of relation type — shall follow the
proposal and review requirements of EFS 2513, clause 7, and shall
record the position that is vacated as well as the position that is
taken.
