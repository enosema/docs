== Worked examples

These examples apply the requirements of this document to four
arrangements that recur in terminology work. The concepts are drawn
from ordinary data-management vocabulary; the lessons are general.

=== Example 1: a clean single-criterion rake

A register maintains a system of data-resource concepts. The
superordinate concept is `data set`. The register subdivides by the
dimension `currency` (the degree to which the data reflect the current
state of the phenomena):

* `current data set` — a data set whose values reflect the current
state;

* `historical data set` — a data set whose values reflect states of
the past; and

* `forecast data set` — a data set whose values represent expected
future states.

The rake passes T1 (three concepts, one rake, criterion recorded),
T2 (one criterion: currency), and T3 (the intensions are disjoint by
the temporal reference of the values: present, past, expected future).
Coverage (T6) is derivable: every data set holds values of some
temporal reference, and the three values partition the possibilities
under the system's boundary convention that a data set mixing
references is out of scope. The example shows the minimum passing
arrangement: one criterion, disjoint intensions, derived coverage.

The relation records of the rake carry the arrangement:

----
relation:
  type: generic
  subject: data-set:current-data-set
  object: data-set
  system: data-resource-system
  qualification:
    criterion: currency
    normative_force: essential
    source: EFS-DM-0012, decision 2026-04-07
    status: confirmed
----

with `historical-data-set` and `forecast-data-set` asserted
identically under the same criterion. T9 passes: one incoming
hierarchical relation per concept. T15 passes: every relation carries
the full qualification.

=== Example 2: a mixed rake and its re-dimensioning

The same register proposes to subdivide `data set` by content type
and by currency at once:

* `current observation data set`;

* `current aggregate data set`;

* `historical observation data set`; and

* `historical aggregate data set`.

The rake fails T3: separating `current aggregate data set` from
`historical observation data set` requires two criteria. The failure
is the arrangement defect that E-6 prohibits. Remediation follows the
re-dimension path of M-9: the register declares the dimensions
`currency` and `content type`, subdivides separately along each, and
composes the positions — a multidimensional concept system per A-10.
The composed positions are `observation, current`, `observation,
historical`, `aggregate, current`, `aggregate, historical`. Each
dimensional rake passes T3 on its own criterion, and the composition
is tested as a whole under T1.

=== Example 3: cross-system membership

A statistics system and a management system both place `data
quality`. In the statistics system, `data quality` sits under
`statistical metadata` through a generic relation; in the management
system, it sits under `management characteristic` through a generic
relation. The memberships are two complete assertions (A-9), each with its own
criterion and source (Q-1, Q-2). The register asserts a mapping
relation `equivalence` between the two positions (E-10, A-11), with
source citations on both sides and the degree recorded (Q-8). T10
passes: the membership pairs and the mapping agree. T14 passes: the
mapping degrees agree in both systems. The example shows that one
concept can hold two truths, one per system, connected by an explicit
correspondence — and that neither system inherits the other's
position.

The cross-system records:

----
# statistics system
relation:
  type: generic
  subject: data-quality
  object: statistical-metadata
  system: statistics-system
  qualification:
    criterion: statistical-process-characteristics
    normative_force: essential
    source: EFS-STAT-0004, review 2026-02-19
    status: confirmed

# management system
relation:
  type: generic
  subject: data-quality
  object: management-characteristic
  system: management-system
  qualification:
    criterion: management-object-characteristics
    normative_force: essential
    source: EFS-MGT-0009, decision 2026-05-21
    status: confirmed

# mapping between the positions
relation:
  type: mapping
  subject: data-quality@statistics-system
  object: data-quality@management-system
  qualification:
    degree: equivalence
    source: EFS-STAT-0004 / EFS-MGT-0009, joint review 2026-09-03
    status: confirmed
----

=== Example 4: a boundary failure and its convention

A register subdivides `delivery time` (the duration from order to
delivery) into `immediate delivery`, `short-term delivery` and
`long-term delivery`, with ranges "under one day", "one to seven
days", "more than seven days". T5 fails: a delivery of exactly one
day is assignable to two coordinates — the open boundary. Remediation
follows the re-boundary path of M-9: the register adopts the
convention that ranges are closed on the lower bound and open on the
upper bound, records the convention in the rake (M-7), and re-tests.
The definitions of the coordinates are amended per EFS 2522 to state
the convention, and the change follows change management (M-10). The
example shows that MECE failures are often boundary failures, and
that the fix is a stated convention, not a redesigned system.

=== Example 5: the residual that was avoided

A register subdivides `identifier` by mechanism into `sequential
identifier` and `random identifier`, then meets an identifier scheme
that is neither sequential nor random in generation. The tempting fix
is a third coordinate `other identifier`. The register resists
(E-6, M-6): a residual category would trade a coverage failure for a
permanent limitation. Instead the re-criterion path of M-9 applies:
the criterion is restated as `generation mechanism` with coordinates
`assigned identifier` and `derived identifier`, into which the
existing scheme and the two original coordinates fall by derivation.
The rake passes T3 and T6 without a residual. The example shows the
order of preference in M-9 doing real work: the weaker remediation
would have shipped a rake that every future new scheme would grow.

=== Example 6: a multidimensional composition end to end

The register restructures the mixed rake of Example 2 as a composed
system. The system description declares two dimensions:

* `currency` — the temporal reference of the values: `current`,
`historical`, `forecast`; and

* `content type` — the form of the data: `observation`, `aggregate`.

Each dimension carries its own rake of coordinate concepts, each
asserted under `data set` through a generic relation whose
qualification names its criterion (Q-2):

----
# dimension: currency
relation:
  type: generic
  subject: data-set:current-data-set
  object: data-set
  qualification:
    criterion: currency
    normative_force: essential
    source: EFS-DM-0012, decision 2026-04-07
    status: confirmed

# dimension: content type
relation:
  type: generic
  subject: data-set:observation-data-set
  object: data-set
  qualification:
    criterion: content type
    normative_force: essential
    source: EFS-DM-0026, decision 2026-10-15
    status: confirmed
----

The composed positions are the coordinate pairs:
`observation/current`, `observation/historical`,
`observation/forecast`, `aggregate/current`,
`aggregate/historical`, `aggregate/forecast`. The system description
declares the excluded combination `forecast/aggregate` — aggregates
are computed from observed data and are never forecasts — which is
the coverage statement of M-5 for the composition. The register
introduces the composed concepts, each carrying its two dimensional
memberships, and the MECE tests run per dimension (T1 to T3 pass on
each; T6 passes with the exclusion statement). The conversion from
the failed flat rake is recorded per M-10, with the flat rake's
relations deprecated and the composition recorded as the replacement.

A consumer of the system reads the composed position of any concept
from its dimensional memberships without inspecting prose (A-15),
and a reviewer re-derives any position by the placement procedure
per dimension. The example closes the loop: the structure that fixed
the overlap is the structure that the tests, the records and the
review all speak in.
