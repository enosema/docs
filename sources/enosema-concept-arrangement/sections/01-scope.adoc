== Scope

This document specifies requirements for the arrangement of concepts
in concept systems. It covers:

* the principles of concept arrangement by subdivision, including
criteria of subdivision, dimensions, and the construction of
hierarchies from generic and partitive relations;

* system membership: the decision procedure by which a concept
becomes a member of a concept system, the record that the decision
produces, membership in one system, and membership in several systems
at once, whether by multidimensional composition or by cross-system
placement;

* the establishment of the relationships that position a concept in a
system: the selection of relation types from the ES relation
vocabulary (EFS 2510), the direction and locus of each assertion, and
the record of each assertion;

* the qualification of established relationships: the source, criterion,
delimitation, presence and count constraints, normative force, and
lifecycle status that a relationship carries; and

* mutual exclusivity of subdivision and collective exhaustiveness: the
tests that verify that every subdivision separates its coordinate
concepts and accounts for the full extension of the superordinate
concept, together with the remediation paths that apply when a test
fails.

This document applies to concept systems maintained in registers that
conform to EFS 2513 and that record concepts, localizations and
relations in machine-readable form per EFS 2516.

This document does not specify:

* the theory of concepts and of concept formation, which ISO 704
covers;

* the structure of the concept entry itself, which EFS 2522 covers;

* the semantics of individual relation types, which EFS 2510 covers;
or

* the operating practice of a register — proposal forms, decision
records, review cadence — which EFG 2519 covers.

In this document, the verb *shall* expresses a requirement on a
conforming register or on the operator of such a register, the verb
*should* expresses recommended practice from which deviation is
permitted when the deviation and its reason are recorded, and the verb
*may* expresses permission.
