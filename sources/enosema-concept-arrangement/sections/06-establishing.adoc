== Relationship establishment (Group E)

=== General

Relationship establishment is the act of connecting two concepts with
a relation. The act has three parts: selecting a relation type from a
vocabulary, directing the relation between its ends, and recording the
assertion. Group E governs the three parts for the relations that
carry arrangement — hierarchical relations — and states the
discipline for the relations that annotate arrangement — associative
and mapping relations.

=== Selection of relation type

E-1:: Every relationship shall use a relation type defined in the ES
relation vocabulary (EFS 2510) or in a vocabulary that the register
declares as an extension of it under EFS 2513, clause 5. Relation
types defined ad hoc in a proposal shall not be asserted.

E-2:: The relation type shall be selected by the semantic test of
EFS 2510 for that type: a generic relation is asserted where the
object concept's intension subsumes the subject concept's intension; a
partitive relation is asserted where the subject concept names a part
of the object concept; an associative relation is asserted where a
non-hierarchical thematic connection exists and neither hierarchical
type applies.

E-3:: Where two candidate relation types both fit, the register shall
assert the more specific type where one exists, and shall record the
reason where the choice is not determined by the vocabulary.

=== Hierarchical relations

E-4:: A generic relation shall be asserted only where the generic
concept and the specific concept are concepts of the same kind: the
specific concept is a narrowing of the generic concept, obtained by
adding characteristics to the generic intension (ISO 704).

E-5:: A partitive relation shall be asserted only where the part
concept satisfies the part-whole test of ISO 704: the parts are
separable in thought from the whole, the parts are essential to the
whole, and each part is of a kind that the whole can contain.

E-6:: A rake of coordinate concepts shall be produced by one
criterion of subdivision. Where the coordinate concepts of one
superordinate concept are produced by two criteria, the rake shall be
split into two rakes along declared dimensions, per clause A-10, and
the two rakes shall be tested separately under Group M.

E-7:: A hierarchical relation shall not skip levels: where concepts
B and C are asserted as superordinate concepts of A, and B is a
superordinate concept of C, one of the two assertions shall be
removed; the register shall not carry both the direct and the
transitive placement.

=== Associative relations

E-8:: An associative relation shall be asserted where a thematic
connection exists that no hierarchical relation expresses, between
concepts at the same or different positions in the system (EFS 2510;
ISO 1087-1). The association shall not substitute for a hierarchy:
where the concepts stand in a generic or partitive relation, the
hierarchical relation is required, and the associative relation is
optional.

E-9:: An associative relation shall be recorded with the thematic
connection stated in its qualification (clause <<qualifying>>): the
statement names the connection — cause, producer, product, instrument,
opposition, or a domain-specific connection — so that a reviewer can
test it.

=== Mapping relations

E-10:: A mapping relation between positions of different systems
shall be asserted only where the positions denote related concepts
whose correspondence the mapping type of EFS 2510 expresses:
equivalence, narrowness, broadness, overlap or dissociation.

E-11:: A mapping relation shall not carry arrangement weight: the
presence of a mapping relation shall not imply membership in the
other system. Membership follows clause <<membership>> only.

=== Direction and locus

E-12:: Relations shall be asserted in the direction that the relation
type of EFS 2510 defines: from specific to generic, from part to
whole, and from subject to object for associative and mapping types as
the type defines. The register shall store the assertion once, in one
direction, and shall derive the inverse view from the type definition
rather than asserting the inverse.

E-13:: A relation between two concepts shall be asserted in the
system in which both concepts are members. A relation between concepts
of different systems is a mapping relation per E-10, not a hierarchical
relation.

E-14:: A relation shall be asserted between concepts, not between
designations and not between entries in particular languages. The
language-specific view of a relation is derived through the
localizations of its concepts (EFS 2504).

=== Relations across editions and versions

A register evolves, and its relations evolve with it. Group E binds
relation evolution to the record so that consumers can follow:

* *assertions survive edition changes.* A relation asserted in one
edition carries into the next unchanged unless a change management
decision modifies it. The relation record carries the dates of
assertion and of last review (EFS 2516), and the arrangement view of
an edition shows the relations as they stood at that edition.

* *deprecation keeps the trace.* A relation that no longer holds is
deprecated with its replacement stated where one exists (E-16, Q-10).
A deprecated generic relation whose superordinate concept was
restructured carries a pointer to the relation that superseded it, so
that a consumer citing the old position can resolve the new one.

* *assertion provenance is per assertion.* Two relations of the same
type between the same pair of concepts in different systems are
different assertions with different sources: the equivalence of
positions across systems does not merge their evidence.

=== Relations and localizations

Relations connect concepts (E-14), and the localized entries of those
concepts inherit the relations through concept identity (EFS 2504).
Three localized cases need care:

* *a relation that holds in one language view only.* Some thematic
connections are language-bound: two concepts that oppose in one
language do not oppose in another where the target concepts differ.
Where the difference is real, the concepts are not identical, and the
register records distinct concepts with a mapping relation — not one
concept with a language-specific relation.

* *a designation-level correspondence.* The relation between the
designation of one language and the designation of another is not a
concept relation; it is the equivalence question of EFS 2504, settled
by the equivalence degree of the localization. The register does not
assert concept relations from designation similarity.

* *script and writing-system variation.* Converted forms of a
designation (ISO 24229) carry no relation information at all; they
are localizations of one designation of one concept. The arrangement
is untouched by script conversion.

=== Record of the assertion

E-15:: Each established relationship shall be recorded per EFS 2516
with: the relation type, the subject and object concept identifiers,
the direction as stored, the register or system in which the relation
is asserted, and the qualification metadata of clause <<qualifying>>.

E-16:: A relationship shall be asserted once and only once per
relation type and pair of concepts in one system. Duplicate
assertions of the same type between the same concepts in the same
system shall be merged; the merged record retains the union of the
qualifying metadata.
