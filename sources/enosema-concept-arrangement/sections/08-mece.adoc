== Mutual exclusivity of subdivision (Group M)

=== The MECE property

A subdivision satisfies the MECE property when it satisfies both of
its parts:

* *mutual exclusivity*: no concept satisfies the intension of more
than one coordinate concept of the rake; and

* *collective exhaustiveness*: the union of the extensions of the
coordinate concepts covers the extension of the superordinate
concept.

The first part separates; the second accounts. A rake that fails the
first part makes the system ambiguous — a consumer cannot tell which
designation applies to an object that falls in the overlap. A rake
that fails the second part makes the system incomplete — objects
exist that the system cannot describe. Group M states the tests for
both parts, the evidence each test admits, and the remediation that
applies when a test fails. Annex B gives the test suite in checklist
form.

=== Tests for mutual exclusivity

M-1:: The intensional disjointness test: for each pair of coordinate
concepts in a rake, the intensions shall be disjoint — no
characteristic set that satisfies both intensions exists. The test is
performed by constructing candidate characteristic sets that satisfy
both intensions; the absence of a construction, demonstrated in the
review record, satisfies the test.

M-2:: The extensional disjointness test: for each pair of coordinate
concepts, the register shall account for at least one object or
boundary statement that separates the extensions, whether by a
delimiting characteristic pair, by a measured boundary, or by a
stated convention that assigns every boundary object to exactly one
coordinate.

M-3:: The single-criterion test: each rake shall be produced by one
criterion of subdivision (E-6). The test names the criterion and
verifies that every coordinate concept is differentiated from its
coordinates by that criterion alone; where differentiating a pair
requires a second criterion, the rake fails the test.

M-4:: The coordinate designation test: where the criterion of
subdivision is expressed in the designations of the coordinate
concepts, the expression shall be uniform across the rake (EFS 2522,
Group S). A rake whose designations express the criterion
inconsistently signals an arrangement defect even where the intensions
are disjoint.

=== Tests for collective exhaustiveness

M-5:: The coverage test: for every rake, the register shall record the
statement of coverage — the claim that every object satisfying the
superordinate intension satisfies at least one coordinate intension —
with the evidence: a derivation, a cited source, or the presence of a
residual category.

M-6:: The residual category rule: where coverage cannot be
demonstrated by derivation or source, the rake shall include a
residual category whose definition states that it holds what the
other coordinates do not hold. A residual category is a last resort:
its presence in a rake is recorded as a known limitation in the
system description, and it shall not be used where a positive
criterion is available.

M-7:: The boundary convention rule: where the extension of the
superordinate concept is continuous and the rake cuts it — sizes,
ranges, durations — the rake shall carry the boundary convention that
assigns every boundary value to exactly one coordinate. A rake that
leaves a boundary value assignable to two coordinates fails
exclusivity; a rake that leaves a boundary value assignable to none
fails exhaustiveness.

=== Failure detection and remediation

M-8:: The register shall run the mechanical subset of the MECE tests
— the tests of Annex B that run over relation records without expert
judgement — as part of publication validation (EFS 2513, clause 10).
A rake that fails a mechanical test shall not be published until the
failure is remediated or a waiver is recorded by the control body.

M-9:: The remediation paths for a failing rake are, in order of
preference:

* *re-criterion*: restate the criterion of subdivision so that the
coordinates become disjoint, retaining the intended coverage;

* *re-dimension*: split the rake into rakes along declared dimensions,
restructure as a multidimensional subdivision (A-10), and retest each
rake;

* *re-boundary*: adopt or restate the boundary convention (M-7) that
removes the overlap or the gap;

* *re-scope*: narrow the superordinate intension so that the failing
objects leave the subdivision, recording the scope change in the
system description; and

* *residual*: add a residual category (M-6), with the limitation
recorded.

M-10:: A remediation that changes intensions or scopes touches the
definitions of published concepts and follows the change management
requirements of EFS 2513, clause 7, before publication.

=== Classes of overlap

Reviewers diagnose exclusivity failures faster when they recognize
the class of overlap. Five classes recur, each with its own signature
and its own remediation:

* *criterion mixing.* Two coordinates differ along one criterion and
share a second criterion unequally — the coordinates of a mixed rake.
Signature: the regrouping test cannot state each coordinate as
"`superordinate + criterion-value`" alone. Remediation: re-dimension
(M-9, A-10).

* *boundary ambiguity.* The coordinates partition a continuous
extension and a value sits on the seam. Signature: the extension walk
finds an object that both coordinates claim. Remediation: re-boundary
(M-7, M-9).

* *intension entanglement.* One coordinate's intension presupposes a
characteristic that the other coordinate excludes, but a third
characteristic smuggles the exclusion back in — the coordinates are
not independently defined. Signature: the disjointness argument for
the pair relies on a characteristic that belongs to neither
delimiting set. Remediation: re-criterion or re-author the defective
definition under EFS 2522, clause 7.

* *residual leakage.* A residual category has grown a positive
intension — "`other X`" now names a recognizable class — and the
class competes with a positive coordinate. Signature: objects are
consistently assigned to the residual although a positive coordinate
exists for them. Remediation: promote the leaked class to a positive
coordinate by derivation, or re-state the residual as an explicit
catch-all with no positive definition.

* *scope drift.* The coordinates are disjoint within the original
scope, but the system has grown and the superordinate's extension has
widened past what the rake covers. Signature: coverage fails on
objects the system did not originally intend to describe.
Remediation: re-scope (M-9) — narrow the superordinate intension or
extend the rake by derivation.

The classes are diagnostic aids, not tests: the tests of Annex B
decide, and the class names the remediation path.

=== Overlap diagnosis procedure

When a rake fails a MECE test, the reviewer works the diagnosis in
this order:

. *Reproduce the failure as an object statement.* State the failing
object — real or constructed — and the two coordinates that claim it.
A failure that cannot be stated as an object statement is a test
artifact, not an arrangement defect.

. *Identify the characteristics in conflict.* Name the
characteristics that pull the object toward each coordinate. The
conflict set is usually short: one or two characteristics carry the
overlap.

. *Classify the overlap.* Match the conflict to the five classes. The
class proposes the remediation path.

. *Choose the remediation.* Apply the order of preference of M-9
against the class: re-criterion, re-dimension, re-boundary, re-scope,
residual. The first path that resolves the failure without damaging
sibling rakes is taken; damage to siblings is checked by re-running
the suite (M-12).

. *Record the diagnosis.* The finding records the object statement,
the conflict characteristics, the class, the chosen path, and the
re-test outcome. The record is the evidence that the published rake
once failed and was fixed — which matters to consumers who adopted
the pre-fix arrangement.

=== MECE and definitions

The MECE tests consume definitions. A rake that passes its tests
under defective definitions passes falsely: disjointness argued from
an incomplete intension, coverage claimed over an extension the
definitions never bound. The dependency runs in one direction: entry
quality (EFS 2522) is a precondition of arrangement quality, not a
consequence. The register reviews the definitions of a failing rake
under EFS 2522, Groups N and C, before remediation is chosen, because
a remediation designed around a defective definition is a remediation
that the next definition revision will invalidate. Conversely, a rake
that passes under sound definitions can still fail later when a
definition is revised: the revision triggers re-test under V-4.

=== MECE in multidimensional systems

In a multidimensional concept system (A-10), the MECE property holds
through composition, and the tests apply at the dimensional rakes:

* *dimensional exclusivity.* Each dimensional rake passes T1 to T5 on
its own criterion, exactly as a single-system rake does. The composed
positions inherit exclusivity from their dimensions: a composed
position is unique because each of its coordinate values is unique
within its dimension.

* *composition coverage.* The composed system covers the superordinate
extension where every dimension covers: an object that each dimension
classifies has exactly one composed position. The system description
states excluded combinations — pairs of coordinate values that the
domain does not admit — and the coverage claim runs subject to that
statement (M-5).

* *dimension independence.* Exclusivity across dimensions is not
required and does not make sense: two dimensions may produce
coordinates that share characteristics, because they classify
different things about the same object. The single-criterion test
(T2) applies within a dimension and says nothing across dimensions.
A reviewer who finds "overlap" between coordinates of different
dimensions has compared along the wrong axis; the finding is the
missing statement of which dimension the rake belongs to, and the fix
is the criterion record (Q-2), not a re-arrangement.

* *conversion to composed form.* A flattened rake that fails T3 is
re-dimensioned (M-9): the dimensions are named, the coordinates are
regrouped per dimension, the dimensional rakes are tested, and the
composed positions replace the flat ones. The conversion changes
records and follows M-10.

=== MECE across the arrangement

M-11:: The MECE property is a property of each subdivision, not of the
whole system at once. A system is MECE when every one of its rakes
passes the tests. The register shall be able to enumerate its rakes —
from the relation records, per A-15 — and to state for each rake
which tests it has passed and when.

M-12:: Rakes at different depths are independent for the MECE tests:
a rake passes or fails on its own criterion, not on the criterion of
its parent rake. Where a parent rake fails and is remediated, the
child rakes are retested, because re-criterion and re-scope change
the extensions that the children subdivide.

M-13:: The coordinate concepts of a rake may themselves be
subdivided along different criteria than each other: a rake formed by
size may contain one coordinate subdivided further by colour and
another by material. Subdivision criteria need not be uniform across
sibling subtrees; uniformity of criterion is required within a rake
only (E-6).
