== Placement decision procedure

This annex gives the working procedure that decides the position of a
concept in a concept system. The procedure applies when a concept
enters a system, when a placement is contested (A-5, A-7), and when a
reviewer re-derives a position as the review instrument (V-5). Each
step states the inputs it consumes and the record it produces.

=== Step 1: State the intension

Write the intension of the candidate concept: the set of
characteristics that its definition supplies. Where the definition is
absent or defective, the placement is premature; the entry returns to
authoring under EFS 2522, and the placement decision waits for a
definition that passes the completeness and clarity criteria of that
document.

=== Step 2: Enumerate candidate superordinates

Enumerate the concepts of the system whose intensions the candidate
intension presupposes: the candidates are the concepts under which the
candidate adds characteristics without removing any. Mechanical
tooling can propose candidates from the structured characteristics of
the register where characteristics are recorded as data; otherwise
the proposer enumerates from the system view.

=== Step 3: Test each candidate

For each candidate superordinate concept, perform three tests and
record the outcome:

* *the subsumption test*: every characteristic of the candidate
superordinate intension appears in the candidate concept intension.
Where one does not, the candidate is not a superordinate concept of
the concept being placed.

* *the differentiation test*: at least one delimiting characteristic
of the candidate concept distinguishes it from every existing
coordinate concept under the candidate superordinate. Where the
candidate cannot be distinguished from a coordinate, the candidate and
that coordinate are the same concept, and the placement resolves to
entry deduplication, not placement.

* *the criterion test*: the distinguishing characteristics all
express the one criterion of subdivision of the rake. Where a
characteristic expresses a different criterion, the candidate rake is
not the right rake; the correct rake is one step through the other
criterion (A-10), or the rake is mixed and fails M-3.

=== Step 4: Select and record

Select the position. Where exactly one candidate passes the three
tests, that position is taken. Where several candidates pass, apply
the tie-breakers in order and record the tie-break used:

* *the immediate-superordinate rule*: prefer the candidate whose
intension the candidate concept most directly narrows — the one that
adds the fewest characteristics between itself and the concept being
placed. Deeper ancestors remain reachable through the hierarchy.

* *the system-scope rule*: prefer the candidate that lies within the
declared scope of the system (EFS 2513, clause 5); an equally correct
position outside the system scope is recorded as a cross-system
membership candidate (A-11), not as a second position.

* *the control-body rule*: where the tie survives, the control body
decides and the decision record cites the candidates and the
tie-breaker outcome (EFG 2519, Annex B).

=== Step 5: Assert the membership

Assert the membership relation with the full metadata of Group A and
Group Q: relation type (E-4 or E-5), superordinate concept, criterion
of subdivision, source, normative force, and status `proposed`. The
position becomes public at `confirmed` (Q-3).

=== Step 6: Test the affected rakes

Run the MECE tests of Annex B on every rake the placement affects:
the receiving rake gains a coordinate, and its exclusivity and
coverage are re-tested (M-11, M-12). A failing rake is remediated per
M-9 before publication.

=== Placement worksheet

The worksheet records one placement decision. The register keeps the
worksheet as part of the proposal record.

[cols="2,5",options="header"]
|===
|Field
|Content

|Concept
|identifier and designation of the concept being placed

|Intension
|the characteristic set, as recorded in the entry

|Candidates
|the enumerated candidate superordinates

|Subsumption
|pass or fail per candidate

|Differentiation
|pass or fail per candidate, naming the delimiting characteristics

|Criterion
|the criterion of subdivision that the passing characteristics
express

|Selected position
|superordinate concept and relation type

|Tie-breaker
|the rule applied where several candidates passed

|Rejected candidates
|the remaining candidates and why they lost

|MECE outcome
|rake tests run and their results

|Decision record
|link to the proposal and decision records
|===

=== Worked micro-example

The procedure in six steps, applied to the placement of `forecast
data set` (the data set of expected future values) in the data-resource
system of Annex D:

. *Intension*: "`data set whose values represent expected future
states of the phenomena`" — the definition supplies the
characteristics: data set (presupposed) and expected-future temporal
reference (added).

. *Candidates*: `data set` (root), `current data set`, `historical
data set` (existing coordinates).

. *Tests*: `data set` passes subsumption — the intension presupposes
it. It passes differentiation — expected-future reference
distinguishes the concept from current reference and past reference.
It passes the criterion test — the distinguishing characteristic
expresses `currency`, the declared dimension. `current data set`
fails subsumption: its intension carries present-state reference,
which the candidate does not presuppose. `historical data set` fails
subsumption likewise.

. *Selection*: one candidate passes; the position under `data set`
along `currency` is taken. No tie-breaker is needed.

. *Assertion*: a generic relation is asserted (E-4) with criterion
`currency`, force `essential`, source citing the proposal, status
`proposed`; it goes to review and ships at `confirmed` (Q-3).

. *Rake test*: the receiving rake now holds three coordinates; T1, T2
and T3 pass — the three temporal references are disjoint, and T6
passes by derivation over the declared boundary convention. The
worksheet records the two failed candidates and the subsumption
results, which is the evidence a later reviewer reads when the rake
is questioned.

The worksheet outcome for this placement, filled from the steps
above, is exactly the record that A-5 and V-5 consume when a reviewer
re-derives the position months later: the derivation reproduces the
tests, and the recorded outcomes let the reviewer confirm rather than
re-argue.
