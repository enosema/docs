[[arrangement]]
== Concept arrangement

=== Principles of arrangement

Concept arrangement gives a body of concepts its shape. The shape is
not decoration: the position of a concept carries information that the
definition alone does not carry, because the position states which
superordinate concept the concept refines, which criterion of
subdivision separates it from its coordinates, and which concepts
stand beside it as coordinates. A consumer who reads a definition
learns the intension of one concept; a consumer who reads a position
learns how the domain is organized.

Arrangement rests on four principles, which the requirements of
clauses <<membership>> to <<validation>> apply.

* *Intension governs position.* A concept belongs where its intension
fits: under the superordinate concept whose intension it presupposes,
and beside the coordinates from which its delimiting characteristics
distinguish it (ISO 704). Position is decided by characteristics, not
by designations, document history, or convenience.

* *One criterion per subdivision.* Each subdivision of a
superordinate concept applies a single criterion of subdivision to
produce its rake of coordinate concepts (ISO 704). Subdividing along
two criteria at once produces coordinates that overlap in extension
and that cannot be separated by definition.

* *Dimensions are declared.* Where several criteria genuinely apply,
each criterion is a dimension: it is named, it is stated as the
criterion of subdivision for its own subdivision, and the coordinate
concepts of the different dimensions are composed into positions of a
multidimensional concept system rather than flattened into one rake.

* *Systems are many.* One concept can occupy one position in each of
several systems. The systems are distinct artifacts with distinct
scopes, and the membership of a concept in each is recorded separately
(<<membership>>).

=== The shape of an arrangement

The requirements of this document are neutral about representation:
they apply to hierarchies of generic and partitive relations, to
multidimensional systems, and to thesaurus-like arrangements in which
broader and narrower relations are complemented by associative ones
(ISO 25964-1). Three shapes recur, and the register states which shape
each system has:

* *a generic hierarchy*, in which every non-root position is reached
from its superordinate concept by a generic relation and every rake
subdivides along one declared criterion;

* *a partitive hierarchy*, in which every non-root position is
reached from its superordinate concept by a partitive relation and
the presence and count constraints of the partitive relations are
recorded (clause <<qualifying>>); and

* *a mixed system*, in which generic and partitive subdivisions
alternate and associative relations connect positions across the
system without carrying arrangement weight.

Associative relations do not place concepts. A position is reached
only through hierarchical relations; associative relations annotate
the arrangement (EFS 2510).

=== Depth and breadth

Depth and breadth of an arrangement are quality trade-offs, not
targets. A deep system with many single-child subdivisions hides its
structure; a wide rake with dozens of coordinates buries its criterion.
The register should observe the following practice, and records any
deviation with its reason:

* a subdivision with a single coordinate concept is collapsed: the
coordinate and the superordinate are merged, or a second criterion
produces further coordinates;

* a rake with more than about twelve coordinate concepts is examined
for a second dimension: where the rake is really the composition of
two criteria, the system is restructured as a multidimensional
subdivision; and

* the depth of a generic hierarchy follows the depth of the intension
hierarchy: each level adds at least one delimiting characteristic, and
a level that adds none is removed.

These are recommended practices. Group M tests, not depth or breadth
limits, decide whether an arrangement is correct.

=== Arrangement mechanics

The principles become concrete through three mechanics that reviewers
apply to every arrangement decision.

*The intension-to-position derivation.* The intension of a concept
determines its position: the concept sits under the concept whose
intension it presupposes, and beside the concepts from which its
delimiting characteristics distinguish it. The derivation is
repeatable — two reviewers applying the derivation to the same
intension reach the same position, or the difference between them is
itself a finding (a defective definition, a mixed rake, a missing
intermediate concept). The validation requirements of
clause <<validation>> make the repeatable derivation the instrument
of review: positions are audited by re-deriving them.

*The regrouping test.* The test of ISO 704 for a subdivision is
applied at construction: the characteristics used to differentiate
the coordinate concepts are regrouped under the criterion of
subdivision; a rake passes when every coordinate can be stated as
"`superordinate + criterion-value`" and nothing else. A coordinate
whose intension needs a second characteristic family to separate it
from its neighbour is the symptom of a mixed rake (E-6), and the
regrouping attempt exposes it before publication.

*The extension walk.* For each rake, the reviewer walks the extension
of the superordinate concept with one question: for an arbitrary
object of that extension, which coordinate takes it? The walk
exercises exclusivity (the object has exactly one answer) and
coverage (an answer exists). Where the answer is "it depends" —
a boundary case, a hybrid, a context-dependent case — the rake is
incomplete without a convention (M-7) or a residual (M-6), and the
walk names the case precisely. The extension walk is a review
technique (Annex B, T4); it is also the fastest way to explain an
arrangement defect to its author, because it states the failure in
terms of objects instead of terms of logic.

=== Systems and their scopes

A concept system exists for a scope: the set of concepts the system
undertakes to arrange. The scope is declared in the system description
(EFS 2513, clause 5) and does three pieces of work in arrangement:

* *it bounds membership*: a concept outside the scope is not placed in
the system even where a position can be constructed for it (A-8
holds a concept unplaced rather than placing it badly; the scope
settles whether the system is the right home at all);

* *it anchors coverage*: the coverage statement of a rake (M-5) runs
over the scope — a rake covers the extension of its superordinate
concept as far as the system scope extends; and

* *it disciplines cross-system choice*: where two systems can both
host a concept, the scope statement, not convenience, decides which
membership is primary, and the other system relates through mapping
(A-11) rather than duplication.

A system without a declared scope cannot pass the coverage tests,
because there is no boundary over which coverage can be claimed. The
register validates scope presence at publication (V-1).

=== The arrangement as evidence

A published arrangement is evidence about the domain: it states, in
machine-readable form, which concepts the community distinguishes and
how the distinctions relate. The evidentiary character raises the
stakes of the requirements in this document. A registry that
publishes a rake with an overlap is not merely untidy; it asserts to
every consumer that two incompatibles are both true of some objects.
The validation requirements of clause <<validation>> exist because
the arrangement publishes assertions, and assertions need the same
gate as definitions.

=== The criterion of subdivision in practice

The criterion of subdivision is the unit of honesty in an
arrangement. Every requirement that names a criterion — A-2, E-6,
M-3, Q-2 — is a requirement that the arrangement state its reason:
why these coordinates and not others. In practice the criterion does
three jobs at once, and a criterion statement is adequate only when
it serves all three:

* *it explains the rake*: a reader of the criterion sees why the
coordinates differ from one another — "`currency`" explains
current, historical and forecast; and

* *it bounds the rake*: a reader can tell what does not belong — a
concept whose distinguishing characteristic is not a form of the
criterion is evidence of a misplaced concept, not of a missing
coordinate; and

* *it survives subdivision*: a child rake states how it refines the
parent criterion — size subdivides by range, colour by hue — and the
chain of criteria is the system's account of itself.

A criterion that does only the first job is a label. The registers
that the corpora exhibit most often stop at the label; the
requirements of this document are calibrated to move practice from
the label to the instrument.

=== What arrangement is not

Arrangement is not classification for retrieval. A register may tag
concepts with facets, subject codes or collection markers for search
and navigation; those tags are not system membership, do not carry
relation semantics, and are not governed by this document. The
register keeps the two mechanisms distinguishable in its records:
membership is a relation assertion with full Group A and Group Q
metadata, and a retrieval tag is a data category outside the relation
set. A system that treats retrieval tags as membership silently loses
the tests of Group M, because nothing forces the tags to satisfy
exclusivity.
