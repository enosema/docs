[[localization-fields]]
== Localization records

=== General

A localized concept is managed as a record that states where the
localization belongs: its language, its script, and — where the
localization is produced by conversion between writing systems — the
writing system conversion system that produced or governs it.

The three fields are cumulative. A localized form of Mandarin Chinese
romanized into Latin script is identified by its language, the Latin
script, and the conversion system (for example, Hanyu Pinyin); a localized
form of Japanese rendered in Hepburn romanization is identified by its
language, the Latin script, and the Hepburn conversion system. The
conversion system field prevents the most common failure of
multilingual interchange: two romanizations of the same term that cannot
be reconciled because the system that generated them is unrecorded.

=== Language field

The language of a localization shall be recorded as an ISO 639-3
three-letter code.

ISO 639-3 is required rather than the two-letter codes of ISO 639-1
because terminology work regularly distinguishes language varieties that
ISO 639-1 collapses: the codes for Mandarin (`cmn`), Cantonese (`yue`),
and Min Nan Chinese (`nan`) exist only in ISO 639-3, and terminology for
the same script or domain frequently differs among these varieties.

[NOTE]
====
Where a consumer requires ISO 639-1 codes, the mapping shall be recorded
alongside the ISO 639-3 code; the ISO 639-3 code remains the record of
authority.
====

[[localization-fields-script]]
=== Script field

The script of a localization shall be recorded as an ISO 15924 code
wherever either of the following holds:

. the language is rendered in more than one script in the scope of the
localization; or

. the localized form is a romanization, transliteration, or transcription
of a form in another script.

Otherwise the script field is optional.

=== Writing system conversion system field

Where a localized form is produced by conversion between writing systems,
the localization record shall cite the writing system conversion system
that produced or governs the conversion, by the identifier of its entry in
the ISO 24229 register.

The cited conversion system shall be registered in the ISO 24229 register
at the time of citation. A conversion system that has been withdrawn from
the register may be cited only as part of a historical record, with its
status recorded.

[example]
The localized form *北京* (Mandarin Chinese, Han script, `cmn-Hans`) has a
romanized form *Beijing*. The localization record for *Beijing* carries
the language `cmn`, the script `Latn` (ISO 15924), and the ISO 24229
identifier of the Hanyu Pinyin conversion system.

[example]
A Japanese localization set comprising 東京 (Jpan), 東京 in Hepburn
romanization *Tōkyō* (Latn), and a kunrei-shiki variant *Tôkyô* (Latn)
records two distinct localized forms with the same language and script,
distinguished solely by their ISO 24229 conversion systems.
=== Record summary

The localization record of a localized concept carries, at minimum:

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
|Field
|Requirement

|language
|ISO 639-3 code; mandatory.

|script
|ISO 15924 code; mandatory where <<localization-fields-script>> applies,
optional otherwise.

|conversion system
|ISO 24229 register identifier; mandatory for localized forms produced
by conversion between writing systems, forbidden otherwise.
|===

The record summary does not replace the terminological content of the
localization — designations, definitions, sources — which is specified in
EFS 2501 and ISO 10241-1.
