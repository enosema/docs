[[skos-projection]]
== SKOS projection profile

=== Projection mappings

Where a dataset is projected into SKOS, the relation mappings of
EFS 2510 apply: hierarchical relation types project to
`skos:broader`/`skos:narrower`, associative types to `skos:related`,
and mapping types to their corresponding `skos:exactMatch`,
`skos:closeMatch`, `skos:broadMatch`, `skos:narrowMatch`,
`skos:relatedMatch` properties.

Concepts shall project to `skos:Concept` resources with:

. `skos:prefLabel` from the preferred designation of each language;

. `skos:definition` from the definition content; and

. `skos:inScheme` referencing the dataset's concept scheme, identified
by the dataset URN.

=== What a projection may lose

A SKOS projection is lossy by construction. A conforming projection may
omit: coordination method (generic, partitive, instantial distinction),
inverse pairing, n-ary relations and their metadata (completeness,
presence, count, delimiting status, criterion), registration metadata,
per-record source citations, and lifecycle relations.

A projection shall not alter semantics: it shall not merge concepts, not
redirect hierarchical projections into associative projections, and not
present `skos:prefLabel` from non-preferred designations.

=== Round-trip prohibition

ES data shall not be round-tripped through SKOS alone: the full record
format of <<concept-records>> is the only interchange-complete form.
Publishers shall retain the full dataset alongside any SKOS projection
and shall not treat the projection as the record of authority.

Where a SKOS property exists that corresponds to an ES relation type,
the projection may carry it as an annotation on the projected concept
(for example, `skos:exactMatch` for the `exact_match` type); the ES
type identifier remains the record of authority in the full dataset.
