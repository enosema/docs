[[operations]]
== Operation of concept management

=== Concept creation

==== Proposal

A concept shall be created through a proposal to the control body stating
the proposed designations, the definition, the placement in the concept
system (superordinate concept and criterion of subdivision), the initial
relations, and the sources.

==== Definition drafting

Definitions of concepts shall be drafted in accordance with ISO 704 and
ISO 10241-1:

* a definition shall describe the intension of the concept, stating the
delimiting characteristics that distinguish it from its coordinate
concepts;

* a definition shall not begin with an article, shall not be a restatement
of the term, and shall not include requirements or advice;

* where a concept is an extensional subdivision (for example, an
exhaustive partitive decomposition), the organization shall verify that
the members exhaust the extension before publishing the definition
(ISO 704; EFS 2510).

==== Review and acceptance

No concept shall be published without review and acceptance by the
control body. The review shall verify at least:

. the correctness of the placement in the concept system and the stated
criterion of subdivision;

. the uniqueness of the concept within the system: the proposal does not
duplicate an existing concept;

. the relation set: initial relations use the controlled vocabulary
(<<relations-mgmt>>) and their directions and inverses are consistent;

. the terminological entries: designations and definitions conform to
ISO 10241-1; and

. the sources: claims are supported by cited sources.

=== Concept record content

The organization shall maintain, for each published concept, a concept
record (<<term-concept-record>>) that carries at least:

. a stable identifier per the identifier scheme (<<planning>>);

. one or more designations, with term type and normative status
(ISO 12620 data categories);

. the definition, with its sources;

. the relations of the concept, recorded per EFS 2510;

. the lifecycle status of the concept; and

. the history of the record: what changed, when, and by whose decision.

Concept records shall be available in machine-readable form
(<<support>>).

[[change-mgmt]]
=== Change management

==== Changes to published concepts

A change to a published concept — definition, placement, relations, or
designation status — shall go through proposal, review, and acceptance,
except for corrections that do not alter the intension or extension of
the concept, which the register manager may apply and record directly.

==== Extending, splitting, merging and instantiating

For each of the following operations, the control body shall decide and
the record shall capture the decision:

* *extending*: adding subordinate concepts under an existing concept; the
criterion of the new subdivision shall be recorded
(<<planning>>);

* *splitting*: replacing one concept with two or more concepts; each
successor shall be linked to the predecessor with the lifecycle relation
`supersedes`/`superseded_by` (EFS 2510), and the predecessor record shall
be retained, not deleted;

* *merging*: replacing two or more concepts with one; the successor shall
link to each predecessor with `supersedes`/`superseded_by`, and the
predecessors shall be retained;

* *instantiating*: promoting an individual concept from a general concept,
or recording an instance of a general concept; the
`instance_of`/`has_instance` relation shall be recorded.

In every operation, identifiers of retired records shall remain resolvable.
Consumer references shall not break.

==== Retirement

A concept shall not be deleted from a published system. Retirement shall:

. set the lifecycle status of the concept to retired or deprecated;

. record, where a successor exists, the relation to the successor; and

. retain the record, its identifiers, and its history.

==== Continuity of references

The organization shall ensure that, across every operation of this
clause, references to its concepts remain resolvable: an identifier
issued at publication resolves for the operational life of the
organization's published records, either to the current record or to the
retired record with its successor information.

[[registration]]
=== Registration

Where the organization operates or participates in a register under
ISO 19135 procedures and the FERIN framework, it shall:

. operate the submission, review, acceptance, publication, and
maintenance procedures of the register;

. align its lifecycle statuses with the ISO 19135 registration statuses
(proposed, accepted, notAccepted, withdrawn, superseded, retired);

. record register events (acceptance, replacement, retirement) both on
the register item and, as lifecycle relations, on the concept record; and

. publish the register so that human and machine consumers can obtain
records and statuses.
