[[planning]]
== Planning of the concept system

=== Breadth and depth

For each concept system it maintains, the organization shall plan:

* *breadth*: the subject fields and domains the system covers, and the
boundary between systems;

* *depth*: the levels of subdivision the system carries, justified by the
needs of the consumers identified in <<organization>>, and not by
accidental authoring history.

The organization should record, for each subdivision level, the criterion
of subdivision that generates it (ISO 704; EFS 2510). A subdivision
without a recorded criterion is a nonconformity at review
(<<evaluation>>).

=== Extending the concept system

When the organization extends a concept system — adding domains, levels,
or criteria — it shall:

. determine the criterion of subdivision for the extension and record it
before subordinate concepts are created;

. assess the impact on existing concepts: parallel subdivisions for
multidimensionality (ISO 704; EFS 2510), not reclassification of
existing concepts unless the control body decides it; and

. record the decision of the control body on the extension.

=== Planning lifecycle and versioning

For its concepts the organization shall plan:

* the lifecycle states concepts pass through (for example, proposed,
accepted, published, deprecated, retired), aligned with ISO 19135
registration statuses where a register is operated;

* the versioning policy for concept records: what constitutes a new
version of a concept, what constitutes a new concept, and how references
remain valid across versions;

* the review interval for each concept system (<<evaluation>>).

=== Planning identifiers

The organization shall plan its identifier scheme for concepts, concept
records, and relation records before publication, so that:

. identifiers are stable, unique, and independent of terms and labels;

. identifiers do not encode lifecycle state, version, or location; and

. consumers can cite an identifier and resolve it to the current record
and, where supported, to the record as of a past date.

NOTE: The FERIN framework's separation of the concept plane and the
content plane is a suitable basis for this planning: identifiers on the
concept plane outlive revisions; content plane items carry their own
status and validity.
