[[corpus]]
== Corpus evidence

=== Methodology

The evidence base of this document is a sample of the ISO Online
Browsing Platform terminology extraction — 406 898 machine-generated
concept files in the record format of EFS 2516, covering ISO and
ISO/IEC/IEC documents from 1983 to 2026, bilingual (English, French),
with one file per concept carrying its managed record and localized
concepts.

From this population a random sample of 3 000 files was analysed,
yielding 4 662 localized concept entries with designations. The sample
statistics below are the empirical reference for the requirement
groups: where the corpus performs well, this document sets its
requirements at or above the corpus level; where the corpus
systematically fails, the corresponding requirement is mandatory and
the failure is catalogued as an anti-pattern (Annex <<anti-patterns>>).

The analysis script is deterministic (fixed sample seed) and is
re-runnable against any termbase in the same record format, so the
same statistics can be produced for an ES register and compared
directly with the ISO baseline.

=== What the corpus teaches

Five lessons set the shape of the requirement groups:

. *The machine floor is high and should be locked in.* Source coverage
(98,8 %) and grammar annotation (98,9 %) show that machine-enforced
fields reach near-universality. The corresponding requirements
(C-1, C-2, D-4) are mandatory with no derogation.

. *Silent absence is the dominant failure.* Empty definitions (1,2 %)
and missing examples (97,1 % absence) never produce complaints; they
produce silent misunderstanding. Completeness requirements therefore
prefer explicit presence over implicit quality.

. *Legacy decay is real.* The empty-definition defects concentrate in
documents from 1983–2012. A register that imports legacy corpora must
re-validate rather than trust conversion (EFG 2519, clause 11).

. *The median definition is short.* Sixteen words is the international
working norm; the rubric anchors definition length accordingly
(<<scoring>>), and verbosity is scored down, not up.

. *Discipline beats heroics.* The corpus's 80,3 % genus-first rate was
not achieved by talent but by convention. The requirement groups below
are written to be checkable in seconds, because rules that take
minutes get skipped.

=== Document-era profile

Structural defects concentrate by document era. The sampled entries
group into three eras with sharply different profiles:

[cols="1,2,1,1,1",options="header"]
|===
|Era
|Example source documents
|Empty-definition rate
|Article-head rate

|Legacy (1983–1997)
|ISO 5843 series, ISO 5702
|2,7 %
|4,8 %

|Transitional (1998–2014)
|ISO 6707-1 (2004), ISO 921 (1997)
|1,6 %
|3,4 %

|Modern (2015–2026)
|ISO 10286 (2021), ISO 24765, IEC/IEEE 81000 series
|0,4 %
|1,9 %
|===

The pattern is monotonic: structural discipline improves with every
era, consistent with the growing adoption of ISO 10241-1 drafting
rules and machine-first extraction. The implication for registers is
direct — imports of legacy content carry a predictable defect load and
require era-aware validation effort.
