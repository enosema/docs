[[corpus]]
== Corpus evidence

=== Methodology

The evidence base of this document is a sample of the ISO Online
Browsing Platform terminology extraction — 406 898 machine-generated
concept files in the record format of EFS 2516, covering ISO and
ISO/IEC/IEC documents from 1983 to 2026, bilingual (English, French),
with one file per concept carrying its managed record and localized
concepts.

From this population a random sample of 3 000 files was analysed,
yielding 4 662 localized concept entries with designations. The sample
statistics below are the empirical reference for the requirement
groups: where the corpus performs well, this document sets its
requirements at or above the corpus level; where the corpus
systematically fails, the corresponding requirement is mandatory and
the failure is catalogued as an anti-pattern (Annex <<anti-patterns>>).

The analysis script is deterministic (fixed sample seed) and is
re-runnable against any termbase in the same record format, so the
same statistics can be produced for an ES register and compared
directly with the ISO baseline.

=== What the corpus teaches

Five lessons from the corpus set the shape of the requirement groups.

. *The machine floor is high, and the requirements lock it in.* Source
coverage (98,8 %) and grammar annotation (98,9 %) show that
machine-enforced fields reach near-universality in practice. The
corresponding requirements (C-1, C-2, D-4) are mandatory with no
derogation.

. *Incomplete entries fail silently.* Empty definitions (1,2 %) and
missing examples (97,1 % absence) do not produce complaints; they
produce silent misunderstanding. The completeness requirements
therefore require explicit presence over implicit quality.

. *Defects concentrate in legacy documents.* The empty-definition
defects concentrate in documents from 1983–2012. A register that
imports legacy corpora re-validates them rather than trusting the
conversion (EFG 2519, clause 11).

. *The median definition is short.* Sixteen words is the international
working norm; the rubric anchors definition length accordingly
(<<scoring>>), and verbosity reduces the score.

. *Convention, not talent, produced the corpus discipline.* The
80,3 % genus-first rate of the corpus follows from drafting convention,
and the requirement groups are written to be checkable in seconds,
because a rule that requires minutes of review is not applied.

=== The IEV corpus

A second corpus — the International Electrotechnical Vocabulary (IEV,
IEC 60050, maintained by IEC TC 1) — provides the multilingual
counterpoint: 22 229 concepts in the Glossarist record format, of which
a 2 000-concept sample was analysed. The IEV is the deepest
multilingual terminology register in operation, and its profile differs
from the ISO corpus in two decisive ways.

*Multilingual breadth.* IEV concepts carry a median of eleven
languages each (mode 11; range 2–19), against the ISO corpus's two
(English, French). Language coverage per concept is the IEV's own
quality dimension: an entry's value scales with the number of
communities it serves.

*The term-only localization model.* IEV's definitions live in the
authoritative languages — English and French definitions are present on
100 % of sampled entries — while every other language localizes the
*designations only*: German, Spanish, Chinese, Arabic, Italian and
Polish localizations carry terms (with normative status, and grammar
where the language model requires it — German 97 %, French 98 %,
Polish 30 %) but no definitions. Russian shows the only partial
pattern (7 % carry definitions).

=== The two localization models

The ISO corpus and the IEV corpus together demonstrate that two
legitimate localization models coexist in international practice:

* *definition-bearing model* — each localization carries its own
definition, with equivalence degrees recorded per EFS 2504 (the model
assumed by Group C of this document); and

* *term-only model* — the authoritative-language definition serves all
localizations, which carry designations (with grammar and status) but
no definitions.

Neither model is defective. A register shall declare which model it
operates, and completeness is judged against the declared model:
under the term-only model, the absence of a localized definition is not
a completeness defect, and the quality burden concentrates on the
authoritative-language definition and on the fidelity of the localized
designations (grammar, status, and the ISO 24229 conversion-system
fields of EFS 2504 for any converted forms).

This distinction refines criterion C-5 and requirement C-R8: their
obligations apply to *definition-bearing* localizations; term-only
localizations conform by designation completeness.
