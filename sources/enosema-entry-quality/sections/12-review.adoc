[[review-process]]
== Review process

=== Roles

Two kinds of reviewer apply the scoring model:

* *term convenor*: owns a register or a document's terminology;
decides grade outcomes, arbitrates criterion disagreements, and owns
the disposition of findings; and

* *reviewer*: performs criterion scoring on proposed or sampled
entries and drafts the findings.

=== When scoring happens

. *At proposal review*: every proposed entry is scored before the
control-body decision (EFS 2513, 7.1.3; EFG 2519, clause 7). The
proposal's score sheet attaches to the proposal thread.

. *At periodic review*: a sample of published entries is re-scored
(EFS 2513, 11.2), sized to detect drift (the sample of the corpus
analysis of clause <<corpus>> is the model: hundreds of entries give
stable medians).

. *On import*: entries imported from legacy or third-party corpora are
scored before acceptance; the corpus evidence of clause <<corpus>>
predicts where imports fail (empty definitions, missing examples).

=== The review workflow

. *Pre-check*: completeness gate first (cc1): an entry missing
mandatory elements is returned immediately with the missing elements
named, without criterion scoring (the flags of <<mandatory-fix-flags>>
make this arithmetic, not judgement).

. *Criterion scoring*: the reviewer walks Annex A's checklist in
order (designation → definition → completeness → clarity →
consistency), scoring each criterion against the anchors of
Annex <<rubric-cards>>. Findings are written per failed criterion,
quoting the entry's own words.

. *Flags check*: the mandatory-fix flags are checked; any flag caps
the grade.

. *Score computation*: per <<scoring>>; the score sheet is produced
(template in Annex <<rubric-cards>>).

. *Disposition*: the term convenor decides:
accept (grade B or A, findings recorded), accept-with-findings
(grade C, findings become improvement items per EFG 2519 clause 12),
or return (grade D, with the failed criteria as the fix list).

. *Re-score*: a returned entry is re-scored on resubmission; the score
sheets of both rounds are retained (EFS 2513, 10.3).

=== Disagreement and calibration

Where two reviewers differ by more than one band, the term convenor
arbitrates against the anchors, and the outcome is recorded in the
register's calibration notes. Registers that score continuously
should run an annual calibration exercise: the same ten entries scored
by all reviewers, differences discussed, anchors amended only by
decision record (EFG 2519, Annex B).

=== Score sheets as records

Score sheets are controlled records (EFS 2513, 10.3): they carry the
entry identifier, the criteria scores, flags, the computed grade, the
reviewer(s), the date, and the disposition. The register's grade
distribution — the set of score sheets over time — is the primary
input to the monitoring metrics of EFG 2519, clause 11.

=== Calibration worked example

Two reviewers, one entry, the disagreement pattern that calibration
exists for:

|nc1 intension

|3 — the delimiting characteristic the second reviewer wanted is in
the notes; the definition correctly stays minimal (N-R10). Anchor
clarification recorded: notes-delimited content does not reduce nc1.

|kc4 notes quality
|1
|2
|1 — the note argues the definition's case ("`this term is preferred
because…`"); K-6 violation. Anchor clarification recorded: advocacy
notes score 1 regardless of usefulness.

|sc4 mapping honesty
|2
|2
|2 — agreed.
|===

Dimension impact: N differs by 1×0,25×(1/1) → normalized 25 points →
grades B vs A. The arbitrated entry scores A; the two anchor
clarifications are written into the register's calibration notes and
apply from the next scoring onward.

Calibration is done when reviewers' grades converge to within one band
without arbitration on two consecutive exercises — typically two to
three exercises for a new team, one per year thereafter.
