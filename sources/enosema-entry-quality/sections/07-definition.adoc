[[definition-quality]]
== Definition quality (Group N)

=== General

The definition carries the entry. Every other element — designations,
sources, relations — attaches to the claim that the definition makes. This clause
states the rules of definition writing as practiced by ISO 704 and
enforced by DIR2, 16.5.1, and groups them into requirements.

=== Rules

N-R1:: *One concept, one definition.* An entry carries a single
definition in each language. Multiple senses are multiple concepts
with separate entries linked by relations, never a forked definition
(DIR2, 16.5.1 note; ISO 10241-1).

N-R2:: *Genus first.* The definition states the superordinate concept
(genus) first, then the delimiting characteristics that distinguish
the concept from its coordinate concepts (ISO 704; the corpus shows
this as the dominant discipline at 80,3 %).

N-R3:: *The substitution test.* The definition shall be substitutable
for the term in a defining context without loss of meaning or
grammaticality. Where substitution fails, the definition is defective
even if it sounds right.

N-R4:: *No circularity.* The definition shall not use the designation
itself, a grammatical variant of it, or a derivative of it, as a
defining element. The corpus circularity heuristic flags 3,5 % of
entries; review must judge each flag, since legitimate term-mentions
(e.g. naming the thing the concept is about) are not circularity.

N-R5:: *No requirements language.* Definitions describe; they do not
prescribe. `shall`, `should`, `must`, and negated ability forms do not
belong in a definition (DIR2). The corpus shows 0,5 % violations —
each one serious.

N-R6:: *No articles at the head.* A definition does not begin with
"`A`", "`An`" or "`The`" (DIR2, 16.5.1; corpus: 3,1 % violations).

N-R7:: *Self-containedness.* The definition shall be understandable
within the register: concepts used in the definition shall either be
defined in the register or be ordinary language of the domain.
Definitions that resolve only by chasing references are defective
(references may support, not substitute).

N-R8:: *One sentence discipline.* A definition is one declarative
sentence. Beyond two sentences, the content belongs in notes; beyond
roughly 35 words, the reviewer should check for a hidden list or a
missing intermediate concept (corpus p90: 32 words).

N-R9:: *No negation-defining.* A definition states what the concept
is, not (primarily) what it is not; negation may delimit against one
nearby coordinate concept where that delimitation is essential.

N-R10:: *Notes and examples are not definition.* Content that is true
of the concept but not delimiting belongs in notes; instances belong
in examples (DIR2, 16.5.5, 16.5.6). The corpus puts notes in 34,1 %
of entries and examples in 2,9 % — the absence of examples is the
corpus's own judgment call, and registers may profile examples as
mandatory where pedagogy or safety warrants.

N-R11:: *Bilingual fidelity.* Where a localization exists, its
definition states the intension of the concept in that language, with
the equivalence degree and note per EFS 2504. A translation that
subtly shifts the intension without an equivalence note is a defect
in both the localization and the entry.

=== Scoring criteria

The definition criteria scored in <<scoring>>:

nc1:: Intension sufficiency (N-2, N-7): the definition states enough
delimiting characteristics to distinguish the concept from its
coordinates.

nc2:: Substitution test (N-3).

nc3:: Formal discipline (N-4, N-5, N-6): circularity, requirements
language, article discipline, single sentence.

nc4:: Economy (N-8): the definition says what is delimiting and
nothing else; notes and examples carry the rest.

nc5:: Localization fidelity (N-9).

=== Worked checks

Substitution test, applied:

[cols="1,2",options="header"]
|===
|Term
|Definition

|`air draught`
|maximum vertical distance between the waterline and the highest point
of a ship
|===

Substitution: "`The radar measures the *maximum vertical distance
between the waterline and the highest point of a ship* of the
vessel.`" — grammatical, meaning preserved: passes.

Circularity, applied to a defective case:

[cols="1,2",options="header"]
|===
|Term
|Definition

|`terminological entry`
|entry in a terminology collection that contains the terminological
data of one concept
|===

The definition defines "`entry`" by "`entry`" — circular: fails N-4.
A conforming definition grounds the genus outside the term
("`structured record of one concept …`").

=== Anti-pattern fixes, worked

Each anti-pattern of Annex <<anti-patterns>> with its definition-level
fix applied, as reviewer-facing before/after pairs.

Circular (AP-03) — before:

[cols="1,2",options="header"]
|===
|Term
|Definition

|`register manager`
|manager of the register who manages the register's records
|===

After:

[cols="1,2",options="header"]
|===
|Term
|Definition

|`register manager`
|party responsible for the operation of a register, including the
integrity of its records and the execution of its registration
procedures
|===

The genus moves outside the term ("`party`"), and the delimiting
characteristics name the responsibilities instead of re-using the
designation.

Requirements language (AP-05) — before:

[cols="1,2",options="header"]
|===
|Term
|Definition

|`safety valve`
|valve that shall open automatically when the set pressure is
exceeded
|===

After:

[cols="1,2",options="header"]
|===
|Term
|Definition

|`safety valve`
|valve designed to open automatically when the set pressure is
exceeded
|===

"`Designed to`" states the characteristic without prescribing conduct;
the prescription belongs in the normative text of the product standard.

Overflow (AP-08) — before:

[cols="1,2",options="header"]
|===
|Term
|Definition

|`release lifecycle`
|the sequence of stages through which a release passes from first
proposal through drafting, internal review, public review, ballot,
correction handling, publication, maintenance, and eventual retirement,
where each stage has entry and exit criteria recorded by the control
body
|===

After: the definition keeps the intension; the enumeration becomes a
sequential n-ary relation (EFS 2510, 7.4) and the criteria note
becomes a note:

[source,yaml]
----
definition:
- content: sequence of stages through which a release passes from
  first proposal to retirement
notes:
- content: Stage entry and exit criteria are recorded by the control
  body per the register charter.
----

And the stages themselves — proposed, drafted, reviewed, published,
retired — live in a `sequential_relation` file ordered by the criterion
"`process step order`", exactly as EFS 2510, 7.4 prescribes. The
definition is back inside the working band, the enumeration is
machine-checkable, and the entry now participates in the graph instead
of narrating it.
