== Corpus statistics

This annex records the full statistics of the corpus analysis of
clause <<corpus>>. Population: 406 898 concept files from the ISO OBP
terminology extraction; sample: 3 000 files (fixed seed), yielding
4 662 localized entries with designations.

=== Source document concentration

The sampled entries spanned hundreds of source documents. The
documents with the highest absolute defect counts (empty definitions)
were legacy vocabularies of the 1983–2012 period — for example the
ISO 9250 series (2012) and the ISO 5843 series (1985–1997) — while
documents extracted from current publications showed near-zero
structural defects. Document-era correlation is the strongest single
predictor of structural defects in the corpus; conversion-era
validation (EFG 2519, clause 11) targets exactly this population.

=== Reproducing the analysis

The statistics of this annex are produced by a deterministic analysis
(fixed random seed) over the corpus in EFS 2516 record format. The
script ships with the register repository that runs the analysis; re-
running it against an ES register produces directly comparable
statistics, and comparing a register's profile with this annex is the
quantitative form of the clause <<corpus>> evidence review.

=== Sample size and methodology notes

The sample of 3 000 files was drawn with a fixed pseudorandom seed, so
every re-run reproduces the same entries. With 4 662 localized entries
scored analytically, the standard error of the median definition length
is below half a word, and presence-rate estimates carry a 95 %
confidence interval of approximately ±1,3 percentage points — tight
enough that every requirement-floor decision in this document rests on
differences much larger than the measurement noise.

Per-dimension rates for subpopulations (single source documents,
domains) are estimated on smaller sub-samples and carry wider
intervals; the annex reports them as profiles, not floor decisions.

The analysis operates on the EFS 2516 record format only: any termbase
that exports the interchange records of EFS 2516 can be measured with
the identical script, making the ISO corpus figures of this annex a
benchmark that ES registers can be compared against directly.
