== Introduction

=== The question this document answers

Terminologists, standards editors and term reviewers regularly
encounter a proposed entry that appears defective although no rule that
anyone can cite marks it as defective. The established standards answer
parts of the question that such a review raises: ISO 704 gives the
theory of concepts and definitions, ISO 10241-1 gives the entry
structure for standards, and the ISO/IEC Directives, Part 2, give the
drafting rules that ISO and IEC documents apply. None of them provides
the reviewer with a scoring instrument.

This document does. It defines what quality means for a concept entry —
its designation, its definition, its completeness, its clarity, and its
consistency with the rest of the termbase — as grouped requirements with
stable identifiers, and it attaches to them a scoring model that a term
convenor or reviewer can apply consistently.

=== Where the rules come from

The requirements and scoring criteria in this document are synthesized
from three sources, and each rule is traceable to at least one:

* *International practice*. ISO 704 (principles and methods), ISO 1087-1
(vocabulary), ISO 10241-1 (terminological entries in standards), and the
ISO/IEC Directives, Part 2 (drafting rules, clauses 15 and 16 in
particular).

* *The corpus*. A sample of 4 662 localized concept entries drawn from
406 898 machine-extracted entries of the ISO Online Browsing Platform
terminology, covering hundreds of source documents from 1983 to 2026
(clause <<corpus>>). The corpus shows what the international system
actually produces: where it excels (source coverage, grammar annotation,
genus-first discipline), and where it systematically fails (empty
definitions in legacy documents, missing examples, definitional
circularity). Requirements in this document are set so that a termbase
built under them measures well against the corpus — and measurably
better on the corpus's weak points.

* *Registration practice*. The ES concept management system requirements
(EFS 2513), the relation vocabulary (EFS 2510), the interchange format
(EFS 2516), and the register operation guide (EFG 2519), which place
entry quality inside a governed, machine-readable, published system.

=== How to use the scoring

The scoring model of clause <<scoring>> turns review judgement into a
reproducible number. It is deliberately simple: five dimension groups
(designation, definition, completeness, clarity, consistency), about
thirty anchored criteria, each scored 0 to 3 against written descriptors,
with fixed weights and grade bands. A term convenor reviewing a
proposed entry uses the checklist (Annex A) to find what to look at and
the rubric (Annex B) to decide the score; the entry's grade follows
arithmetic, not taste.

Scoring serves three purposes:

* *Review*: a proposed entry below grade C returns to its author with
the criteria that failed marked — review feedback becomes specific.

* *Monitoring*: a termbase's grade distribution over time shows whether
quality is improving (EFS 2513, clause 11).

* *Publication gating*: a register may set a minimum grade for
publication; the score sheet is the record that the gate was applied.

Scoring does not replace expert judgement. The anchors encode the
judgement on which experts already agree; where a criterion cannot be
decided mechanically, the rubric states that limitation, and the
reviewer decides.
