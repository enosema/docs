[[completeness]]
== Completeness (Group C)

=== General

Completeness asks: does this entry carry everything a consumer needs
to *use* the concept with confidence — to cite it, to validate it
against data, to translate it? The corpus gives the machine floor
(sources 98,8 %, grammar 98,9 %) and shows the gaps (empty definitions
1,2 %, examples 2,9 %). The requirement group sets the floor for a
conforming register above the corpus where the corpus fails.

=== Rules

C-R1:: The entry shall carry the elements declared mandatory in
<<anatomy>>: identifier with URN, preferred designation, definition,
at least one domain, sources, and lifecycle metadata.

C-R2:: The definition shall be present and non-empty. The corpus's
1,2 % empty definitions are all defects: an entry without a definition
is a pointer without a referent.

C-R3:: Sources shall cite the origin of the concept and of the
definition content: document, clause where applicable, and link where
the source is resolvable. The corpus's near-universal source coverage
is the standard; entries without sources are defects (contrast with
the conversion-era corpus, where only extraction guarantees them).

C-R4:: Examples shall be present where the register profile requires
them, and otherwise where the concept is commonly misunderstood or
where instances materially aid use. The corpus's 2,9 % example
coverage is a baseline, not a target.

C-R5:: Notes shall be present where the concept is subject to
recurring misunderstanding, boundary questions, or regional variation.
A note is the correct place for information that is true and useful
but not delimiting (N-R10).

C-R6:: Domain assignment shall state the subject field(s) of the
concept, using the register's domain vocabulary.

C-R7:: Lifecycle metadata shall be complete: registration status,
acceptance date, and the history of record changes (EFS 2513, 7.2).

C-R8:: Localizations shall be complete per register policy: each
declared localization present, each carrying the equivalence degree
and, where the degree is not `full`, the equivalence note (EFS 2504).

C-R9:: Relations known to the concept owners shall be present: a
concept imported from another register carries its mapping relations;
a concept with known coordinates carries its placement
(EFS 2510; EFS 2513, 7.1.3).

=== Scoring criteria

The completeness criteria scored in <<scoring>>:

cc1:: Mandatory element presence (C-1).

cc2:: Source quality (C-2): not only present but precise — document,
clause, resolvable link.

cc3:: Illustrative content (C-3, C-4): examples and notes where they
aid use.

cc4:: Localization completeness (C-5).

cc5:: Relation and mapping completeness (C-6).

[example]
Completeness, contrasting two entries for the same concept family:

[source,yaml]
----
# complete entry (abridged)
id: ship-draught
terms: [{designation: draught, normative_status: preferred, type: expression}]
definition: [{content: vertical distance between the waterline and the
              lowest point of a ship}]
sources: [{origin: {ref: {source: ISO, id: ISO 19642}, clause: 3.2.1}}]
domains: [shipping]
examples: [{content: "a vessel with a draught of 12 m cannot enter the
            inner harbour"}]
----

[source,yaml]
----
# incomplete entry (same register, defective)
id: air-draught
terms: [{designation: air draught, normative_status: preferred, type: expression}]
# definition: MISSING
# sources: MISSING
# domains: MISSING
----

The second entry fails C-1 on three elements; under the rubric it
scores cc1 = 0 and cannot achieve a grade above D regardless of the
quality of its designation — completeness failures gate (<<scoring>>).
