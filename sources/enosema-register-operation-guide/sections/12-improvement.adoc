[[improvement-practice]]
== Improvement practice

This clause gives guidance for the requirements of EFS 2513, clause 12.

=== Nonconformity handling

When validation, review, audit, or a consumer surfaces a
nonconformity, work it in five recorded steps:

. *Record*: file it with the finding, its source (validation rule,
audit line, consumer report), and severity.

. *Correct*: fix the affected records; for published records, the fix
is itself a recorded change through the procedures of clause 7 (consumer
reports on identifiers and references are always treated as urgent).

. *Trace the cause*: records, process, or governance? A recurring YAML
quoting defect is a process defect (add the lint); a decision repeatedly
reversed is a governance defect (clarify the charter).

. *Sweep*: check whether the same defect exists in sibling records —
validation sweeps are cheap; manual sweeps target the family of the
defect.

. *Verify*: at the next review, confirm the nonconformity has not
returned.

=== Improvement decisions

Improvements are control-body decisions like any other, recorded with
Annex B's form. The standing inputs:

* monitoring metrics with triggered actions (clause 11);

* review and audit findings;

* consumer requirement changes (clause 4);

* vocabulary and schema evolution — new versions of EFS 2510 and
EFS 2516, and evolution of registered vocabularies the register uses
(such as new ISO 24229 conversion systems entering the localization
fields); and

* toolchain upgrades, which are recorded decisions too: pin versions,
record why, and re-run the full validation before and after.

=== The improvement log

Keep a one-page improvement log: date, source finding, decision
reference, effect, verification date. At the next review, the log is
the checklist: each improvement is verified to have had its effect, and
unverified improvements are carried forward explicitly rather than
silently dropped.

=== Continual improvement, realistically

A register improves when three loops run at once: the *record loop*
(defects found → corrected → swept), the *process loop* (causes →
charter, checklists, automation), and the *content loop* (consumers →
scope → criteria). This guide's structure follows those loops on
purpose; a register that only ever exercises the record loop repeats the same
class of corrections without reducing their cause.

=== Improvement log — worked entries

The improvement log of clause 12, filled with two representative
entries:

[source,yaml]
----
log:
- date: 2026-11-11
  source: build failure in publication run (YAML colon in definition)
  decision: DR-2026-017
  action: >-
    Add the scalar quoting rule to EFS 2516 (clause 4.4); add quoting
    lint expectation to the review checklist of EFG 2519, clause 7.
  effect: >-
    Run fails at lint with file and line before any manual review time
    is spent.
  verified: 2026-11-12 (next run green, lint caught a recurrence)

- date: 2026-11-01
  source: registry could not categorize the register's documents
  decision: DR-2026-012
  action: >-
    Introduce the `guide` doctype end to end: vocabulary spec, record
    front matter, registry category mapping, and site publication.
  effect: >-
    Guides publish in their own registry category; directives and
    standards unaffected.
  verified: 2026-11-11 (deploy verified live)
----

The verified field is the discipline: an improvement without a
verification date is a promise, and the review of clause 11 treats it
as an open item.
