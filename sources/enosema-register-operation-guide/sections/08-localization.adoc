[[localization-ops]]
== Localization operations

Guidance for EFS 2513, clause 8, with EFS 2504.

=== The localization workflow

A localization moves through five states; keep the state visible in the
proposal or issue that carries it:

. *Requested*: a localization is required (by policy, or by a consumer
need recorded against the concept).

. *Drafted*: a translator or domain expert produces the draft
designation(s) and definition.

. *Assessed*: the equivalence degree is assigned (<<equivalence-worksheet>>).

. *Reviewed*: a reviewer checks the terminology method, not just the
language: designation type, normative status, definition intension.

. *Accepted*: the control body or its delegate accepts; acceptance of
any degree other than `full` is a recorded decision with reasons
(EFS 2513, 8.3).

=== Assignment of work

Localization work splits into two different competences, and proposals
should say which is being requested:

* *Translation-drafting* (language competence): producing candidate
designations and definitions in the target language; and

* *Equivalence assessment* (terminology competence): deciding the
degree, writing the equivalence note, and checking concept-system
fit (the localized concept's position in the target-language concept
system may differ).

Assigning both to one person is fine when one person has both
competences; assigning translation to a tool without an equivalence
assessment step is not.

=== Record practice

Per EFS 2504 and EFS 2516, each localization record carries:

* language as ISO 639-3 (`eng`, `fra`, `cmn`, `yue`, …);

* script as ISO 15924 where applicable (multiscript languages,
romanizations);

* the writing system conversion system as an ISO 24229 identifier for
any converted form; and

* the equivalence degree (`full`, `partial`, `none`, `directional`)
and — for anything other than `full` — the equivalence note.

[[equivalence-worksheet]]
=== Equivalence assessment worksheet

Work the assessment in this order and record the outcome at each step:

. *Intension comparison*: state the characteristics of the source
concept and of the candidate target concept. Shared characteristics →
candidate `full`; overlapping but differing → candidate `partial`;
no real correspondence → `none`.

. *Concept-system check*: does the candidate target concept occupy the
same position in the target-language concept system (same superordinate,
same criterion)? If the position differs, the degree drops to at least
`partial`, and the note says which position differs.

. *Directionality check*: does the correspondence hold in reverse? If
the target term means more (or less) than the source term when read
back into the source language, the degree is `directional`, and the
note states the working direction.

. *Conversion check*: for romanized or transliterated forms, name the
ISO 24229 conversion system used; two romanizations of one term are
different localizations.

. *Note drafting*: one to three sentences, stating what differs and
for whom. A note that would not change a translator's behaviour is too
vague.

[example]
Working assessment record:

----
concept: privacy (eng)
candidate: vie privée (fra)
degree: partial
note: >-
  French "vie privée" covers the legal-institutional aspects of privacy
  more strongly than the English concept, which also spans personal
  boundaries; drafting in French legal texts should prefer the narrower
  legal term where the context is regulatory.
----

=== Cadence

Two standing triggers keep localizations from rotting:

* on acceptance of any change to a source concept's intension, open
review tasks for all localizations whose degree is `partial`,
`none`, or `directional` (EFS 2513, 8.4); and

* at the periodic register review (clause 11), sample a few
`full`-degree localizations and re-verify them: degrees drift as
languages drift.

=== A worked localization set

One source concept, two localizations, three different degrees — the
set demonstrates every field in play.

*Source concept record* (`concepts/privacy.yaml`, managed document
abridged):

[source,yaml]
----
id: privacy
data:
  identifier: privacy
  uri: urn:example:concept:privacy
  localized_concepts:
    eng: privacy-eng
    fra: privacy-fra
    de: privacy-de
status: valid
----

*English localization* (the source of authority for the intension):

[source,yaml]
----
---
id: privacy-eng
termid: privacy
data:
  language_code: eng
  terms:
  - type: expression
    designation: privacy
    normative_status: preferred
  definition:
  - content: "The condition of having control over the collection,
    retention, and disclosure of information about an individual."
status: valid
----

*French localization* — partial degree:

[source,yaml]
----
---
id: privacy-fra
termid: privacy
data:
  language_code: fra
  terms:
  - type: expression
    designation: vie privée
    normative_status: preferred
  definition:
  - content: "Aspect de la vie d'une personne soustrait à l'attention
    publique et à l'ingérence d'autrui."
status: valid
equivalence_degree: partial
equivalence_note: >-
  French "vie privée" covers the legal-institutional aspects of privacy
  more strongly than the English concept, which also spans personal
  boundaries; drafting in French legal texts should prefer the narrower
  legal term where the context is regulatory.
----

*German localization* — full degree:

[source,yaml]
----
---
id: privacy-de
termid: privacy
data:
  language_code: de
  terms:
  - type: expression
    designation: informationelle Selbstbestimmung
    normative_status: admitted
  definition:
  - content: "Die Befugnis des Einzelnen, über die Erhebung, Speicherung
    und Verwendung seiner personenbezogenen Daten grundsätzlich selbst
    zu bestimmen."
status: valid
equivalence_degree: full
----

Reviewer notes on the set:

* the French degree is `partial` and carries the mandatory note; its
acceptance was a recorded decision with the reasoning cited;

* the German designation is `admitted`, not `preferred`: the German
legal term is established but the register's review judged the plain-
language German preference still unsettled — designation status and
equivalence degree are independent axes;

* a romanized German form (if one were added) would be a fourth
record, with script `Latn` and the ISO 24229 conversion system named;
it would not be a second German localization of the same content.
