[[localization-ops]]
== Localization operations

This clause gives guidance for the requirements of EFS 2513, clause 8,
and applies the two localization models of EFS 2522 (clause 5) to
register operation, with EFS 2504.

=== Declaring the localization model

A register operates one of two localization models, and the model is
declared in the register charter:

* *the definition-bearing model*: each declared localization carries
its own definition, and equivalence degrees are recorded per EFS 2504
and EFS 2522; and

* *the term-only model*: the authoritative-language definition serves
all localizations, and each localization carries its designations,
grammar, normative status and — for converted forms — its ISO 24229
writing system conversion system, without a localized definition.

The declared model decides what completeness means for the register
(EFS 2522, requirement C-5): under the definition-bearing model, a
localization without a definition is incomplete; under the term-only
model, it is the declared form. The model is changed only by a
decision of the control body, because the change alters the review
workload and the consumer contract of every localization.

=== The definition-bearing workflow

Under the definition-bearing model, a localization moves through five
states, and the state is visible in the proposal or issue that carries
it:

. *Requested*: a localization is required, by policy or by a recorded
consumer need.

. *Drafted*: a translator or domain expert produces the draft
designations and definition.

. *Assessed*: the equivalence degree is assigned
(<<equivalence-worksheet>>).

. *Reviewed*: a reviewer checks the terminology method as well as the
language — designation type, normative status, and definition
intension.

. *Accepted*: the control body, or its delegate, accepts the
localization. Acceptance of any degree other than `full` is a recorded
decision with reasons (EFS 2513, 8.3).

=== The term-only workflow

Under the term-only model, the workflow concentrates on designations
and on the authoritative definition:

. *Designation production*: a language expert produces the localized
designation set, with term types, normative status and grammar
information per the language model of the register.

. *Conversion control*: where a localized form is a romanization or
transliteration, the record carries the target script (ISO 15924) and
the ISO 24229 conversion system that governs the conversion
(EFS 2504; EFS 2522, Group C).

. *Review*: a reviewer checks the designations against the
authoritative definition of the concept, in the authoritative
language; the reviewer confirms that each localized designation can
carry the intension of the concept in its language.

. *Acceptance*: the localization is accepted as an equivalent
designation set, and the entry records the authoritative definition as
the definition of record for all languages.

The term-only model does not remove the equivalence question. A
language expert who finds that no designation can carry the
authoritative intension raises the gap as a recorded finding, per the
resolution steps of the register charter.

=== Assignment of work

Localization work splits into two competences, and proposals state
which competence a task requires:

* *translation drafting* (language competence): producing candidate
designations and, under the definition-bearing model, candidate
definitions; and

* *equivalence assessment* (terminology competence): deciding the
degree, writing the equivalence note, and checking the position of the
concept in the target-language concept system.

Assigning both competences to one person is acceptable when one person
holds both. A translation produced by a tool without an equivalence
assessment step is not accepted under either model.

=== Record practice

Each localization record carries the fields of EFS 2504 and EFS 2516:
the ISO 639-3 language code; the ISO 15924 script, wherever the
language is multiscript or the form is a romanization or
transliteration; the ISO 24229 writing system conversion system
identifier for converted forms; and, under the definition-bearing
model, the equivalence degree and note.

[[equivalence-worksheet]]
=== Equivalence assessment worksheet

Under the definition-bearing model, the assessment is worked in the
following order, and the outcome is recorded at each step:

. *Intension comparison*: state the characteristics of the source
concept and of the candidate target concept. Shared characteristics
indicate a candidate `full` degree; overlapping but differing
intensions indicate `partial`; no real correspondence indicates
`none`.

. *Concept-system check*: the candidate target concept occupies the
same position in the target-language concept system as the source
concept occupies in its system — the same superordinate concept and
the same criterion of subdivision. A different position reduces the
degree to at least `partial`, and the note states which position
differs.

. *Directionality check*: the correspondence holds in reverse only if
the target term denotes the same extension when read back into the
source language. A target term that means more, or less, than the
source term in reverse gives the degree `directional`, and the note
states the working direction.

. *Conversion check*: a romanized or transliterated form names the
ISO 24229 conversion system that produced or governs it; two
romanizations of one term are two distinct localizations.

. *Note drafting*: the equivalence note is one to three sentences that
state what differs and for whom. A note that would not change the
behaviour of a translator is too vague to record.

[example]
The following assessment record shows the outcome fields:

----
concept: privacy (eng)
candidate: vie privée (fra)
degree: partial
note: >-
  French "vie privée" covers the legal-institutional aspects of
  privacy more strongly than the English concept, which also spans
  personal boundaries; drafting in French legal texts should prefer
  the narrower legal term where the context is regulatory.
----

=== Cadence

Two standing triggers keep localizations current:

* a change to the authoritative definition triggers review of the
localizations whose degree is `partial`, `none` or `directional`
under the definition-bearing model, and of the designations of all
localizations under the term-only model, because a changed intension
can leave a designation unable to carry the concept; and

* the periodic register review re-verifies a sample of `full`-degree
localizations, because degrees drift as languages drift.
