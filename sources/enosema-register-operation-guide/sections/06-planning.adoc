[[planning]]
== Planning the register

Guidance for EFS 2513, clause 6.

=== Identifier scheme design

Decide the identifier scheme once, write it down as a rule, and never
break it. Working rules that age well:

. *Separate the planes.* Item identifiers are permanent; editions and
statuses are not part of the identifier. `relation-type/supersedes` is
an identifier; `superseded` is a status; the year is an edition. Do not
bake dates or states into identifiers.

. *One URN namespace per register*, of the form
`urn:<org>:<register>:<collection>:<local-id>`, registered once and
documented on the register site.

[example]
The ES Relation Vocabulary:

[cols="1,1"]
|===
|Item
|URN

|Dataset
|`urn:enosema:relation-vocabulary:2026`

|Relation type item
|`urn:enosema:relation-type:<identifier>`, for example
`urn:enosema:relation-type:broader_generic`
|===

. *Identifiers are for machines and citations, names are for humans.*
Never rename an identifier to improve its readability; improve the
preferred designation instead.

. *Reserve the right to sub-collections.* If the register may grow
collections later (relation types today, designation types tomorrow),
give each collection its own namespace segment from day one, so that
URNs never need to change shape.

=== Edition and versioning policy

Answer three questions in writing:

* *What is an edition?* The usual answer: an edition is a published,
immutable snapshot (register edition, <<term-register-edition>>). Item
editions may differ from dataset editions; record both policies.

* *What is a new version of an item?* The usual answer: a new version
changes the intension (definition, relations, designation status) of
the item; editorial corrections within one publication run are not a
new version.

* *When do references break?* Never, by policy: identifiers resolve
forever (EFS 2513, 7.6.4). Editions give consumers the *as-of* view;
the current view resolves to the latest edition.

=== Planning criteria of subdivision

EFS 2510 requires every subdivision to carry its criterion; in
operation, the practical step is a *criteria register* — a page in the
register repository listing every criterion in use, its wording, and
the concepts it subdivides.

[example]
A criteria register entry:

----
criterion: power-source
wording: "Subdivides transport equipment by its primary energy source."
applies-to: road-vehicle
subdivisions: motorized, human-powered
since: 2026-01
----

When a proposal introduces a new criterion, the reviewer checks the
criteria register first: an existing criterion that produces the same
subdivision means the proposal should join it, not fork it.

=== Planning for breadth and depth

Practical heuristics (EFS 2513, 6.2):

* A subdivision level earns its place when a consumer decision depends
on it, not when a distinction merely exists.

* Two to seven coordinate concepts per subdivision is comfortable for
review; larger sets usually hide a missing intermediate concept or a
missing criterion.

* Plan depth by consumption: every level a consumer must traverse to
use a concept should correspond to a decision they make.

=== Planning publication

Decide at planning time, not at first-release time:

* the publication formats (EFS 2516: CSV, TTL, JSON-LD at minimum);

* the publication cadence (continuous on merge, or scheduled runs);

* the base URL and path of the published site; and

* whether editions are published as separate URLs or as one current URL
with archives.

=== URN resolution design

A URN that cannot resolve is a string. Decide at planning time how
each URN form resolves:

[cols="1,2,1",options="header"]
|===
|URN form
|Resolves to
|Mechanism

|Dataset URN
|The dataset's published page
|Register site routing

|Concept URN
|The concept's page on the published site
|Stable URL derived from the identifier (EFS 2516, 8.3)

|Collection URN
|A listing page or SKOS collection
|Generated pages
|===

Two practices make this durable:

* derive public URLs from identifiers mechanically (one rule, no
exceptions), so the resolution works before any page is hand-tuned;
and

* keep old URLs working when editions change: an edition's pages
remain reachable at their editioned paths even after a new edition
publishes.

=== Edition policy — a worked example

The policy questions of clause 6 answered for a concrete register:

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
|Question
|Answer (recorded)

|What is an edition?
|A publication run that changes the concept set or any intension. The
dataset id is editioned (`<register>-<year>`); runs that only correct
editorial defects refresh the same edition.

|What is a new version of an item?
|A change to the item's definition, relations, or designation status.
Editorial corrections within a run are not a version change.

|When do references break?
|Never. Concept URNs resolve to the current edition; editioned paths
resolve to the edition as published.
|===

The answers are recorded once, cited at every review, and changed only
by decision record — the policy is part of the register's contract
with its consumers, and changing a contract has a decision trail.
