[[governance]]
== Governance in practice

Guidance for EFS 2513, clause 5.

=== Establishing the control body

The control body needs a written charter. Half a page is enough; the
essential contents are:

. *Composition*: how many members, appointed by whom, term length.

. *Quorum and voting*: what counts as a decision; whether the chair
votes; handling of abstentions (EFD 101, balloting practice is a good
default: Yes / No / Abstain, two-thirds Yes for acceptance, "`No`"
votes carry constructive comments).

. *Decision channels*: synchronous (meetings) and asynchronous
(written ballot on the issue tracker); asynchronous ballots should have
explicit opening and closing dates.

. *Records*: where decision records (Annex B) are kept; who writes
them (the register manager is a good default).

. *Appeals*: to whom an appeal is addressed, in what time it is
acknowledged, and who hears it other than the original decision-makers
(EFS 2513, 5.3).

[example]
A minimal charter (working text, adapt freely):

[quote]
____
The X Register Control Body consists of three to five members appointed
by the Board of Y. The chair is appointed by the Board and does not
vote except to break ties. Decisions are taken by written ballot on the
register issue tracker, opened for at least 10 working days, and pass
by two-thirds of votes cast excluding abstentions. Every decision is
recorded by the register manager as a decision record within five
working days. Appeals are addressed to the Board of Y, which hears
them without participation of the members of the original decision.
____

=== Roles in daily operation

Map the roles of EFS 2513 (5.5) onto named practices:

* *register manager*: runs the publication runs (clause 7), writes
decision records, maintains register.yaml, and is the contact point
named on the register site;

* *concept owners*: one per concept domain or subdivision; they draft
proposals and respond to review comments on their concepts;

* *reviewers*: check proposals against the review checklists
(clause 7); at least one reviewer should not be an author of the
proposal under review;

* *submitters*: everyone entitled to open proposals through the
submission channel.

A single small team may hold several roles; the separations that matter
are: the register manager should not be the sole reviewer of their own
proposals (EFS 2513, 5.5), and appeals should not be decided by the
authors of the contested decision.

=== Meeting and ballot practice

Most registers run on asynchronous ballots; meetings are for deadlock
and design discussions. Practice that works:

* every ballot issue carries the proposal link, the review summary, and
the exact question to be voted on;

* ballots state their window (`opens X`, `closes Y`, time zone
included);

* results are posted in the same issue as the ballot, with the decision
record linked;

* meetings, when held, produce minutes whose actions reference ballot
issues — never the reverse.

=== Decision records

Write one decision record per decision (Annex B). The record's value is
in the *why*: a register's decision log is the reason new volunteers
can understand the register without archaeology.

Minimum fields: identifier, date, proposal link, decision
(accepted / returned / rejected / withdrawn), rationale, votes, effects
(records created, identifiers issued, editions affected).

Keep decision records numbered and immutable once closed; corrections
are new records that supersede, never edits.

=== Appeals practice

An appeal is processed in four steps, with dates at each:

. *Acknowledgement* (within the charter's stated time): appeal
received, forwarded to the appeal body.

. *Standing check*: is the appellant affected, is the appeal within
time, does it state grounds?

. *Hearing*: the appeal body reviews the decision record, the proposal,
and the grounds; it may consult the control body but its members who
decided the original matter do not vote.

. *Outcome*: appeal accepted (decision reversed or redone) or
rejected; the outcome is recorded as a decision record referencing the
contested one.

Publish the appeal outcomes summary in the register's periodic review
(clause 11); a register with repeated appeals on the same ground has a
process defect, not a participant problem.

=== Meeting minutes template

Meetings produce minutes whose actions always reference ballot or
proposal issues:

[source,text]
----
Register Control Body — meeting 2026-11-05
Present: <names>
Agenda:
1. Review outcome, proposal #87 — consensus unclear -> ballot #87 opened
   (closes 2026-11-09). Action: manager (DR after close).
2. Criteria register: proposal to add criterion "publication-channel"
   -> owner to draft wording against ISO 704 subdivision principles.
   Action: owner, by 2026-11-19.
3. Intake times trending high -> intake checklist to move to CI template.
   Action: manager, next sprint.
----

=== Asynchronous-first governance

Registers whose participants span time zones run almost entirely on
written ballots; meetings are the exception for deadlock and design
discussions. Two consequences worth planning for:

* the charter's decision channel default should be the written ballot,
with meetings convened only when a ballot fails or a question is
design-level; and

* every ballot thread should be self-contained — proposal link, review
summary, exact question, voting mechanics — because for many members
the thread *is* the meeting.
